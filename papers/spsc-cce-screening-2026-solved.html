English — Vocabulary, Idioms & Grammar

1. English analogy: Atlas : Maps :: Anthology : ?

  • A. Poems
  • B. Stories
  • C. Literature
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Poems. An atlas is a collection of maps; an anthology is a collection of poems.

2. Which of the following word means "The highest point of structure"?

  • A. Acme
  • B. Amnesia
  • C. Anorexia
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Acme. Acme means the highest point or peak.

3. Which of the following word means "intrinsically"?

  • A. In medias res
  • B. Per
  • C. Per se
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Per se. 'Per se' means by itself, intrinsically.

4. Which of the following word means "courtyard or outdoor space"?

  • A. Plaza
  • B. Patio
  • C. Rodeo
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Patio. A patio is a paved outdoor courtyard area.

5. One word substitution: Compulsion to tell lies?

  • A. Mythomania
  • B. Megalomania
  • C. Theocracy
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Mythomania. Mythomania is a compulsion to tell lies.

6. One word substitution: Battle against the gods?

  • A. Theomachy
  • B. Titanomachy

Answer: A — Theomachy. Theomachy is a battle against the gods.

  • A. Anglophile
  • B. Androphile
  • C. Philanderer
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Anglophile. An anglophile loves and admires England and the British.

8. One word substitution: A shrewish loud-mouthed female?

  • A. Virago
  • B. Notorious
  • C. Incorrigible

  • A. Chiropodist
  • B. Chirographer

  • D. None of these

  • A. Esthetics
  • B. Genetics
  • C. Gerontology
  • D. Clinical psychology
Answer: C — Gerontology. Gerontology is the study of the physical, social and mental aspects of ageing.

11. The Antonym of Inchoate is?

  • A. Developed
  • B. Unformed
  • C. Primitive
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Developed. Inchoate means just begun and unformed; its opposite is developed.

12. The Antonym of Recondite is?

  • A. Simple
  • B. Manifest
  • C. Sociable
  • D. Both A and B
Answer: D — Both A and B. Recondite means obscure and hidden; simple and manifest are both opposites.

13. The Antonym of Jejune is?

  • A. Scanty
  • B. Sophisticated
  • C. Plentiful
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Sophisticated. Jejune means dull and naive; sophisticated is the opposite.

14. The Antonym of Antediluvian is?

  • A. Modern
  • B. Ancient
  • C. Defiant
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Modern. Antediluvian means extremely old-fashioned; modern is the opposite.

15. The Synonym of Pulchritude is?

  • A. Wisdom
  • B. Wealth
  • C. Beauty
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Beauty. Pulchritude means beauty.

16. The Synonym of Munificent is?

  • A. Puny
  • B. Generous
  • C. Philanthropic
  • D. Both B and C
Answer: D — Both B and C. Munificent means very generous, so both generous and philanthropic fit.

17. The Synonym of Parsimonious is?

  • A. Stingy
  • B. Honest
  • C. Brave
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Stingy. Parsimonious means stingy or miserly.

18. The Synonym of Lugubrious is?

  • A. Libelous
  • B. Mournful
  • C. Heavy
  • D. Frequent
Answer: B — Mournful. Lugubrious means mournful and gloomy.

19. You have hissed all my mystery lectures (Instead of missed all my history lectures). The above sentence is the example of?

  • A. Homophone
  • B. Spoonerism
  • C. Paradox
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Spoonerism. Swapping first sounds ('hissed my mystery' for 'missed my history') is a spoonerism.

20. Using double negatives. Example: Not bad meaning good. The above is the example of?

  • A. Spoon feeding term
  • B. Litotes
  • C. Malapropism
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Litotes. Litotes understates through a double negative, e.g. 'not bad' meaning good.

21. The scientist's theory was initially dismissed as ______, but later experiments provided compelling evidence for its validity.

  • A. Conventional
  • B. Preposterous
  • C. Redundant
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Preposterous. A theory first dismissed as absurd is preposterous.

22. The philosopher's arguments were so ______ that even seasoned scholars struggled to follow his reasoning.

  • A. Convoluted
  • B. Transparent
  • C. Simplistic
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Convoluted. Arguments that are twisted and hard to follow are convoluted.

23. The artist's work was praised for its ______ quality, blending elements of surrealism with stark realism.

  • A. Monotonous
  • B. Derivative
  • C. Eclectic
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Eclectic. Eclectic means drawing on many styles, here surrealism blended with realism.

24. English analogy: Palindrome : Symmetry :: Paradox : ?

  • A. Contradiction
  • B. Illusion
  • C. Irony
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Contradiction. A paradox is built on contradiction, just as a palindrome is built on symmetry.

25. Change into Passive Voice: "They will have finished the meeting by 5 p.m."

  • A. The meeting has been finished by 5 p.m.
  • B. The meeting will have been finished by 5 p.m.
  • C. The meeting is finished by 5 p.m.
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — The meeting will have been finished by 5 p.m.. Future perfect passive: will have been + past participle.

26. English Analogy: Haiku : Japan :: Sonnet : ?

  • A. Italy
  • B. England
  • C. Greece
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Italy. The sonnet form originated in Italy before spreading to England.

27. Change the Speech: Indirect: The teacher asked the students if they had completed their homework. Which is the correct Direct Speech?

  • A. The teacher said, "Have you completed your homework?"
  • B. The teacher asked, "Had you completed your homework?"
  • C. The teacher asked, "Did you complete your homework?"
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — The teacher said, "Have you completed your homework?". A yes/no question in indirect speech returns to direct form: 'Have you completed your homework?'

28. Change the Speech: Indirect: They exclaimed with joy that they had won the match. Which is the correct Direct Speech?

  • A. They said, "We have won the match!"
  • B. They said, "We won the match!"
  • C. They said, "We had won the match!"
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — They said, "We have won the match!". An exclamation of joy over a completed win: 'We have won the match!'

29. One word substitution: Study of human face?

  • A. Mensuration
  • B. Horology
  • C. Physiognomy
  • D. Sinology
Answer: C — Physiognomy. Physiognomy is the study of the human face.

30. One word substitute: A person who abstains from worldly pleasures for religious reasons is?

  • A. Ascetic

Answer: A — Ascetic. An ascetic abstains from worldly pleasures for religious reasons.

31. Idiom "To sleep with the fishes" means?

  • A. To make absurd excuses
  • B. To be dead
  • C. Always be in a dream world
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — To be dead. 'To sleep with the fishes' means to be dead (killed).

32. Idiom "A load of cobblers" means?

  • A. Rubbish
  • B. Good news
  • C. Very famous
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Rubbish. 'A load of cobblers' is slang for rubbish or nonsense.

33. The idiom "To throw one's hat in the ring" means?

  • A. To surrender in defeat
  • B. To announce one's candidacy or willingness to compete
  • C. To celebrate victory prematurely
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — To announce one's candidacy or willingness to compete. Throwing one's hat in the ring means announcing one's candidacy or entering a contest.

34. If someone is "showing the white feather," they are?

  • A. To show signs of cowardice / Showing cowardice
  • B. Pretending to be innocent
  • C. Signaling surrender in battle
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — To show signs of cowardice / Showing cowardice. Showing the white feather means showing cowardice.

35. The phrase "to ride roughshod over" means?

  • A. To act with extreme caution
  • B. To dominate or treat others harshly without regard / To dominate or treat someone harshly without consideration
  • C. To travel recklessly on horseback
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — To dominate or treat others harshly without regard / To dominate or treat someone harshly without consideration. To ride roughshod over someone is to dominate or treat them harshly without consideration.

36. The phrase "to send someone to Coventry" means?

  • A. To deliberately ignore or ostracize someone
  • B. To punish someone severely
  • C. To promote someone unexpectedly
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — To deliberately ignore or ostracize someone. To send someone to Coventry means to deliberately ignore or ostracise them.

37. The idiom "to throw one's cap over the windmill" means?

  • A. To abandon a task midway
  • B. To act recklessly without considering consequences
  • C. To challenge authority boldly
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — To act recklessly without considering consequences. To throw one's cap over the windmill means to act recklessly without considering the consequences.

38. The idiom "To gild the lily" means?

  • A. To improve something naturally beautiful
  • B. To exaggerate a story
  • C. To waste effort by adding unnecessary ornamentation
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — To waste effort by adding unnecessary ornamentation. To gild the lily means wasting effort by adding ornament to something already beautiful.

Mathematics & Reasoning

39. In a certain code, CAT = 3120, DOG 4157. What will BAT equal?

  • A. 3121
  • B. 2130
  • C. 2120
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — 2120. Letters are replaced by their alphabet positions: C=3, A=1, T=20. So B=2, A=1, T=20 gives 2120.

40. A student scores 60% in English, 70% in Math, and 80% in Science. If the maximum marks of each subject are 100, what is his overall percentage?

  • A. 65%
  • B. 70%
  • C. 75%
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — 70%. The maximum marks are equal, so average the percentages: (60+70+80)/3 = 70%.

41. A train travels 60 km at 60 km/h and then 60 km at 120 km/h. What is its average speed?

  • A. 80 km/h
  • B. 72 km/h
  • C. 100 km/h
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — 80 km/h. Total distance 120 km divided by total time 1.5 hours = 80 km/h.

42. Find the missing number: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

  • A. 42
  • B. 43
  • C. 44
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — 42. The differences grow 4, 6, 8, 10... the next is 12, so 30 + 12 = 42.

43. A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue, and 6 green balls. Two balls are drawn at random. What is the probability both are green?

  • A. 1/5
  • B. 1/6
  • C. 1/7
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — 1/7. P(both green) = C(6,2)/C(15,2) = 15/105 = 1/7.

44. If the 5th term of an arithmetic sequence is 18 and the 15th term is 58, what is the sum of the first 25 terms?

  • A. 1250
  • B. 1300
  • C. 1350
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — 1250. Common difference d = (58-18)/10 = 4 and a = 2; sum of 25 terms = 25/2 x (4 + 24x4) = 1250.

45. A man spends 1/3 of his salary on rent, 1/5 on food, and 1/6 on transport. He still has Rs.600 left. What is his salary?

  • A. Rs. 3000
  • B. Rs.1800
  • C. Rs.2000
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Rs.2000. He spends 1/3 + 1/5 + 1/6 = 7/10 of his salary; the remaining 3/10 is Rs 600, so the salary is Rs 2,000.

46. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2: 3: 4. Find the smallest angle?

  • A. 40°
  • B. 50°
  • C. 60°
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — 40°. The parts total 9, so each part is 20 degrees; the smallest angle is 2 x 20 = 40 degrees.

47. A shopkeeper marks an item 40% above cost price and then offers a discount of 20%. What is the overall profit percentage?

  • A. 11%
  • B. 12%
  • C. 13%
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — 12%. Marked price 140 less 20% discount = 112, a profit of 12% on the cost price 100.

48. A clock shows 3:15. What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand?

  • A. 7.5°
  • B. 0°
  • C. 15°
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — 7.5°. At 3:15 the minute hand is at 90 degrees and the hour hand has moved 7.5 degrees past 3, so the angle is 7.5 degrees.

49. If A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, and C is the mother of D, how is A related to D?

  • A. Uncle
  • B. Brother
  • C. Father
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Uncle. C is D's mother and A is C's brother, so A is D's uncle.

50. Logical Problem Solving: All engineers are intelligent. Some intelligent people are not doctors. Conclusions? I Some engineers are not doctors. II All doctors are intelligent?

  • A. Only I follows
  • B. Only II follows
  • C. Both follow
  • D. Neither follows
Answer: D — Neither follows. Neither conclusion is forced: the statements say nothing certain about engineers or doctors as a whole.

51. Logical Problem Solving: All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. Therefore?

  • A. Some roses fade quickly.
  • B. Some flowers are roses.
  • C. All flowers are roses.
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Some flowers are roses.. 'All roses are flowers' converts validly to 'some flowers are roses'.

52. The diagonals of a rhombus are 12 cm and 16 cm. Find its area?

  • A. 72 cm²
  • B. 96 cm²
  • C. 80 cm²
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — 96 cm². Area of a rhombus = half x d1 x d2 = half x 12 x 16 = 96 sq cm.

Everyday Science & General Science

53. Which principle explains why pressure cookers cook food faster?

  • A. Gay-Lussac's Law
  • B. Boyle's Law
  • C. Dalton's Law
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Gay-Lussac's Law. At constant volume, higher pressure raises the boiling point of water, so food cooks faster.

54. Insulin is produced and secreted by which organ of the body?

  • A. Liver
  • B. Pancreas
  • C. Kidney
  • D. Brain
Answer: B — Pancreas. Insulin is made by the beta cells of the pancreas.

55. The SI unit of energy is?

  • A. Kelvin
  • B. Joule
  • C. Temperature
  • D. Mole
Answer: B — Joule. The joule is the SI unit of energy and work.

56. Which vitamin deficiency leads to scurvy, breakdown of skin, blood vessels and teeth?

  • A. Vitamin A
  • B. Vitamin C
  • C. Vitamin D
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Vitamin C. Scurvy is caused by lack of vitamin C (ascorbic acid).

57. Which phenomenon explains why stars twinkle?

  • A. Reflection
  • B. Refraction in atmosphere
  • C. Diffraction
  • D. Scattering
Answer: B — Refraction in atmosphere. Starlight bends in layers of air of different densities; this atmospheric refraction makes stars twinkle.

58. Which part of the cell contains genetic material?

  • A. Ribosome
  • B. Cytoplasm
  • C. Nucleus
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Nucleus. The nucleus stores DNA, the cell's genetic material.

59. Which law explains why satellites remain in orbit around Earth?

  • A. Newton's First Law
  • B. Newton's Second Law
  • C. Newton's Law of Gravitation
  • D. Kepler's Third Law
Answer: C — Newton's Law of Gravitation. Gravity holds satellites in orbit; their forward motion balances Earth's pull.

60. Which law explains lift in airplane wings?

  • A. Boyle's Law
  • B. Bernoulli's Principle
  • C. Pascal's Law
  • D. Archimedes' Principle
Answer: B — Bernoulli's Principle. Faster air over the wing lowers the pressure above it (Bernoulli's principle), producing lift.

61. Which of the following is used in fire ‘extinguishers’?

  • A. Carbon sulphite
  • B. Carbon monoxide
  • C. Carbon dioxide
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide smothers flames by cutting off oxygen, so it is used in extinguishers.

62. Which enzyme is responsible for breaking down starch into maltose in the human digestive system?

  • A. Lipase
  • B. Pepsin
  • C. Amylase
  • D. Trypsin
Answer: C — Amylase. Amylase (in saliva and pancreatic juice) breaks starch into maltose.

63. A universal donor has the blood group?

  • A. B
  • B. O-
  • C. AB
  • D. A
Answer: B — O-. O-negative blood has no A, B or Rh antigens, so anyone can receive it.

64. Which scientist proposed the heliocentric model of the solar system?

  • A. Ptolemy
  • B. Copernicus
  • C. Galileo
  • D. Kepler
Answer: B — Copernicus. Copernicus proposed that the Sun, not the Earth, is at the centre of the system.

65. Which law explains why ice floats on water?

  • A. Boyle's Law
  • B. Archimedes' Principle
  • C. Pascal's Law
  • D. Bernoulli's Principle
Answer: B — Archimedes' Principle. Ice is less dense than water, so it floats; the floating condition follows Archimedes' principle.

66. The hardness of water is mainly due to the presence of?

  • A. Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions
  • B. Na⁺ and K⁺ ions
  • C. Cl⁻ and SO₄²⁻ ions
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions. Calcium and magnesium salts (Ca and Mg ions) make water hard.

67. Which process releases the most energy per unit mass?

  • A. Fusion of hydrogen nuclei
  • B. Combustion of coal
  • C. Combustion of methane
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Fusion of hydrogen nuclei. Nuclear fusion releases the most energy per unit mass; it powers the Sun.

68. The strongest oxidizing agent among the halogens is?

  • A. Chlorine
  • B. Fluorine
  • C. Iodine
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Fluorine. Fluorine is the strongest oxidising agent among the halogens.

69. Which principle governs the order of orbital filling in atoms?

  • A. Aufbau Principle
  • B. Pauli Exclusion Principle
  • C. Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Aufbau Principle. The Aufbau principle says electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first.

70. Moho discontinuity is found between?

  • A. Inner core and outer core
  • B. Crust and Mantle
  • C. Upper mantle and lower mantle
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Crust and Mantle. The Moho (Mohorovicic) discontinuity separates the crust from the mantle.

71. The faint Young Sun paradox suggests that early Earth should have been frozen. Which factor most likely resolved this paradox?

  • A. Elevated greenhouse gas concentrations
  • B. Higher geothermal heat flux
  • C. Stronger magnetic shielding
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Elevated greenhouse gas concentrations. A stronger early greenhouse effect kept the Earth warm despite a fainter young Sun.

72. The Chandler wobble refers to?

  • A. Variation in Earth's orbital eccentricity
  • B. Irregular precession of Earth's rotation axis
  • C. Shifts in tectonic plate boundaries
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Irregular precession of Earth's rotation axis. The Chandler wobble is a small irregular wobble in the Earth's rotation axis.

73. Which mineral is considered the most abundant in Earth's mantle?

  • A. Olivine
  • B. Feldspar
  • C. Pyroxene
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Olivine. Olivine is the main mineral of the upper mantle.

74. Which isotope ratio is most commonly used to determine the age of Earth's oldest rocks?

  • A. Carbon-14 to Nitrogen-14
  • B. Potassium-40 to Argon-40
  • C. Uranium-238 to Lead-206
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Uranium-238 to Lead-206. Uranium-238 decaying to lead-206 is used to date the Earth's oldest rocks.

75. Which gas is used in fluorescent lamps?

  • A. Neon
  • B. Xenon
  • C. Argon
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Argon. Fluorescent lamps contain argon gas (with mercury vapour).

76. Which concept explains why black holes may eventually evaporate?

  • A. Hawking radiation
  • B. Dark energy repulsion
  • C. Gravitational wave emission
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Hawking radiation. Hawking radiation slowly carries energy away, so black holes may evaporate.

77. What is the theoretical function of a wormhole in space time?

  • A. A region where time stops
  • B. A black hole that emits radiation
  • C. A tunnel connecting two distant points in space time
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — A tunnel connecting two distant points in space time. A wormhole is a hypothetical tunnel joining two distant points in spacetime.

78. What is the Schwarzschild radius of a black hole?

  • A. The radius of the accretion disk
  • B. The radius at which escape velocity equals the speed of light
  • C. The radius where nuclear fusion stops
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — The radius at which escape velocity equals the speed of light. At the Schwarzschild radius, the escape velocity equals the speed of light.

79. Which phenomenon explains why distant galaxies appear to move away faster the farther they are?

  • A. Hubble's law
  • B. Newton's law of gravitation
  • C. Doppler effect
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Hubble's law. Hubble's law: galaxies recede at speeds proportional to their distance.

80. How does the Doppler effect help in echocardiography?

  • A. It amplifies sound waves in arteries
  • B. It measures changes in blood pressure
  • C. It detects frequency shifts in ultrasound waves reflected by moving blood
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — It detects frequency shifts in ultrasound waves reflected by moving blood. Moving blood shifts the frequency of reflected ultrasound; this Doppler shift shows blood flow.

81. Why does MRI use strong magnetic fields?

  • A. To accelerate electrons in blood
  • B. To align hydrogen nuclei in the body
  • C. To heat tissues for imaging
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — To align hydrogen nuclei in the body. The strong field aligns hydrogen nuclei in body tissues so their signals can form an image.

82. Why does a spinning ice skater speed up when pulling arms inward?

  • A. Increase in torque
  • B. Increase in kinetic energy from muscles
  • C. Conservation of angular momentum
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Conservation of angular momentum. Pulling the arms in reduces the moment of inertia, so the spin rises; angular momentum is conserved.

83. Why does a bicycle remain stable when moving but topple easily when stationary?

  • A. Friction increases at higher
  • B. Gyroscopic effect of rotating wheels
  • C. Air resistance stabilizes motion
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Gyroscopic effect of rotating wheels. Spinning wheels give a moving bicycle gyroscopic stability.

84. Why does diabetes mellitus cause frequent urination (polyuria)?

  • A. Glucose reduces ADH secretion
  • B. Insulin increases water excretion
  • C. Glucose increases osmotic pressure in renal tubules
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Glucose increases osmotic pressure in renal tubules. Excess glucose in the kidney tubules holds water by osmosis, increasing urine output.

85. Why does the heart rate increase during exercise?

  • A. Increased vagal stimulation
  • B. Decreased oxygen demand
  • C. Increased sympathetic stimulation releasing norepinephrine
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Increased sympathetic stimulation releasing norepinephrine. Sympathetic stimulation releases norepinephrine, which speeds up the heart during exercise.

86. Which organelle is most directly involved in drug detoxification in liver cells?

  • A. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
  • B. Lysosome
  • C. Mitochondria
  • D. None of these
Answer: A — Smooth endoplasmic reticulum. The smooth endoplasmic reticulum of liver cells detoxifies drugs.

87. Why does alcohol act as a depressant on the nervous system?

  • A. It accelerates nerve conduction
  • B. It blocks dopamine receptors
  • C. It enhances GABA activity
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — It enhances GABA activity. Alcohol enhances GABA, the main inhibitory messenger, slowing the nervous system.

88. Why does the stomach secrete hydrochloric acid?

  • A. To absorb glucose
  • B. To dissolve fats
  • C. To activate pepsinogen into pepsin
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — To activate pepsinogen into pepsin. Hydrochloric acid converts inactive pepsinogen into the enzyme pepsin and kills germs.

89. Why does drinking too much water in a short time cause harm?

  • A. Water increases blood viscosity
  • B. Kidneys stop filtering water
  • C. Excess water dilutes sodium levels in blood
  • D. None of these
Answer: C — Excess water dilutes sodium levels in blood. Too much water dilutes the sodium level in blood (hyponatraemia), which can be harmful.

90. Why does lactic acid build up in muscles during intense exercise?

  • A. Carbon dioxide accumulates excessively
  • B. Oxygen supply is insufficient for aerobic respiration
  • C. Muscle fibers break down proteins
  • D. None of these
Answer: B — Oxygen supply is insufficient for aerobic respiration. When oxygen supply is insufficient, muscles respire anaerobically and lactic acid builds up.

91. Why does butter melt faster than ghee at room temperature?

  • A. Butter has more water content
