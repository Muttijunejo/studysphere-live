Islamiyat Book — Notes & MCQs

Islamiyat · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

This book presents the basic, factual Islamiyat knowledge that screening tests ask: the pillars of Islam, simple facts about the Holy Quran, key events of the Seerah and the Islamic calendar. The notes are brief and respectful, written for quick revision.

The Five Pillars of Islam

  • Shahadah — the declaration of faith: there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad (peace be upon him) is His Messenger.
  • Salah — the five daily prayers: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha.
  • Zakat — giving a fixed share (2.5%) of one's saved wealth, when it reaches the nisab, to the poor and needy.
  • Sawm — fasting from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan.
  • Hajj — the pilgrimage to Makkah, once in a lifetime, for those who are able and can afford it. It is performed in the month of Dhul-Hijjah; its main day, the Day of Arafat, falls on 9 Dhul-Hijjah.

The Holy Quran — Basic Facts

  • The Holy Quran has 114 surahs and 30 paras (juz). The longest surah is Al-Baqarah; the shortest is Al-Kawthar.
  • The first revelation came to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) in Cave Hira; its opening verses were from Surah Al-Alaq, beginning with the word “Iqra” (Read).
  • Surah An-Nasr is commonly described as the last complete surah to be revealed.
  • Revelation came over a period of about 23 years, part in Makkah and part in Madinah. Surahs revealed in Makkah are called Makki and those in Madinah are called Madani.
  • The Quran was collected in book form during the caliphate of Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) and the standard copies were prepared under Hazrat Usman (RA).

Seerah — Key Events

  • The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) was born in Makkah in the Year of the Elephant, commonly dated to about 571 CE. His father Abdullah had passed away before his birth; his mother was Amina.
  • He received the first revelation at the age of 40. For about 13 years he preached in Makkah.
  • In 622 CE he migrated to Madinah; this event is called the Hijrah, and the Islamic calendar is counted from it.
  • The Battle of Badr was fought in 2 AH (624 CE) and was the first major battle of Islam. The Battle of Uhud followed in 3 AH and the Battle of the Trench (Khandaq) in 5 AH.
  • Makkah was conquered peacefully in 8 AH. The Prophet (peace be upon him) delivered his Farewell Sermon during Hajj in 10 AH and passed away in Madinah in 11 AH (632 CE).

Prophets Mentioned in the Quran

The Quran mentions 25 prophets by name. Among them are Hazrat Adam, Hazrat Nuh (Noah), Hazrat Ibrahim (Abraham), Hazrat Ismail, Hazrat Ishaq, Hazrat Yaqub, Hazrat Yusuf, Hazrat Musa (Moses), Hazrat Dawud, Hazrat Sulaiman, Hazrat Isa (Jesus) and the last Prophet, Hazrat Muhammad (peace be upon all of them).

  • Hazrat Ibrahim (AS) is known as Khalilullah (Friend of Allah) and is remembered for building the Kaaba with his son Hazrat Ismail (AS).
  • Hazrat Musa (AS) received the revelation of the Taurat (Torah); Hazrat Dawud (AS) received the Zabur (Psalms); Hazrat Isa (AS) received the Injeel (Gospel).
  • Muslims believe in all prophets and in the revealed books, with the Quran as the final and complete guidance.

Islamic Calendar & Worship Basics

  • The Islamic (Hijri) calendar is a lunar calendar of 12 months, beginning with Muharram and ending with Dhul-Hijjah.
  • Ramadan, the ninth month, is the month of fasting. Shawwal begins with Eid-ul-Fitr, and Eid-ul-Adha is celebrated on 10 Dhul-Hijjah.
  • Jummah (Friday) prayer is offered in congregation at noon in place of Dhuhr.
  • Wudu is the washing performed before prayer. Tayammum is dry ablution with clean earth when water is not available.
  • The call to prayer is called the Azan, and the one who calls it is the muezzin.
