Mathematics HCF & LCM 10 Practice MCQs

Highest Common Factor and Lowest Common Multiple — two of the most repeated topics in SPSC, teaching and screening tests. Short, simple notes in easy English with Roman Urdu explanations, then 10 practice MCQs with an answer key.

HCF and LCM — Short Notes

  • HCF (Highest Common Factor) — the largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly. Roman Urdu: sab se bara mushtarka factor — jo number dono ko poora taqseem kar de. Example: HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
  • LCM (Lowest Common Multiple) — the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. Roman Urdu: sab se chhota mushtarka multiple — jo number dono ka multiple ho. Example: LCM of 12 and 18 is 36.
  • The HCF is never bigger than the smallest number. The LCM is never smaller than the biggest number.
  • If two numbers have no common factor except 1, they are called co-prime. Their HCF is 1 and their LCM is their product. Example: 13 and 17 are co-prime — HCF = 1, LCM = 221.
  • 1 is a factor of every number, but 1 is NOT a prime number. 2 is the smallest prime number and the only even prime.
  • Test trap: HCF answers a "largest number that divides exactly" question; LCM answers a "smallest number divisible by" or "together again after" question.

Method 1: Prime Factorisation

  • Write each number as a product of prime numbers.
  • For HCF: take the primes common to all numbers, with the smallest power. For LCM: take every prime that appears, with the highest power.
  • Example — 24 and 36: 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3, 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3. Common primes: 2 × 2 × 3 = 12, so HCF = 12. All primes at highest power: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 72, so LCM = 72.
  • Check: HCF × LCM = 12 × 72 = 864 = 24 × 36. The rule works.

Method 2: Division Method (for LCM)

  • Write the numbers in a row. Divide them all by the smallest prime that divides at least two of them. Bring down numbers that do not divide. Repeat until no two numbers share a divisor.
  • Example — LCM of 12, 18 and 24: divide by 2 → 6, 9, 12; by 2 → 3, 9, 6; by 3 → 1, 3, 2; by 2 → 1, 3, 1; by 3 → 1, 1, 1. Multiply the divisors: 2 × 2 × 3 × 2 × 3 = 72. So LCM = 72.
  • For HCF by division: divide the bigger number by the smaller one; then divide the divisor by the remainder, again and again, until the remainder is 0. The last divisor is the HCF. Example: HCF of 48 and 36 → 48 ÷ 36 = 1 remainder 12; 36 ÷ 12 = 3 remainder 0, so HCF = 12.

The Golden Rule

HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers
  • If the product of two numbers is 48 and their HCF is 4, then LCM = 48 ÷ 4 = 12.
  • If the LCM is 72 and the HCF is 12, the product of the numbers is 12 × 72 = 864.
  • This rule works only for TWO numbers — never use it for three numbers.

Word Problems — Test Style

  • "Largest number that divides exactly" → find the HCF. Example: largest number that divides 42 and 63 exactly is 21.
  • "Smallest number divisible by" → find the LCM. Example: smallest number divisible by 6 and 8 is 24.
  • "Bells toll together again after" → find the LCM of the time gaps. Example: bells every 6, 8, 12 and 18 minutes toll together again after 72 minutes.
  • "Largest square tile to cover a floor" → the tile side is the HCF of the floor's length and width.
