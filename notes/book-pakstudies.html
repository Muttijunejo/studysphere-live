Pakistan Studies Book — Notes & MCQs

Pakistan Studies · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

This book covers the Pakistan Studies questions that screening tests repeat most: the Pakistan Movement year by year, the geography of the country, key points of the 1973 Constitution and national symbols. Dates and facts are given in short lines so they are easy to memorise.

Pakistan Movement — Key Timeline

  • 1857 — The War of Independence: the peoples of the subcontinent rose against British rule and were defeated; it is remembered as the first great struggle for freedom.
  • 1906 — The All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka (30 December 1906) to protect the political rights of Muslims.
  • 1930 — Allama Muhammad Iqbal, in his presidential address at Allahabad, presented the idea of a separate Muslim state in the north-west of India.
  • 1940 — The Lahore Resolution (also called the Pakistan Resolution) was passed on 23 March 1940 at Lahore, under the leadership of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah. It demanded independent states for Muslims in their majority areas.
  • 1947 — Pakistan came into being on 14 August 1947. Quaid-e-Azam became its first Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan its first Prime Minister.

Geography — Provinces, Rivers & Mountains

  • Pakistan has four provinces: Sindh, Punjab, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, plus Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. The capital is Islamabad.
  • Balochistan is the largest province by area; Punjab is the largest by population.
  • The Indus is the longest and most important river of Pakistan. Its main tributaries include the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej.
  • K2 (8,611 m), in the Karakoram range, is the second-highest mountain in the world. Nanga Parbat stands in the Himalayas.
  • Pakistan shares borders with China, Afghanistan, Iran and India, and has a coastline on the Arabian Sea where Karachi and Gwadar ports are located.

Constitution of 1973 — Key Points

  • The Constitution of 1973 is the present, supreme law of Pakistan. It was passed by the National Assembly in April 1973 and came into force on 14 August 1973.
  • It declares Pakistan a federal parliamentary republic and Islam as the state religion.
  • It gives every citizen fundamental rights, such as equality before law, freedom of speech and freedom of religion, within the limits of law.
  • Parliament (Majlis-e-Shoora) has two houses: the National Assembly and the Senate. The President is the head of state and the Prime Minister is the head of government.
  • The Objectives Resolution of 1949, passed under Liaquat Ali Khan, is a key early document: it stated that sovereignty belongs to Allah and that the state would run on democratic principles with protection for minorities.
  • Urdu is the national language; the Constitution provides that arrangements be made for it to be used for official purposes alongside English during the transition period.

National Symbols & Facts

  • National language: Urdu · National flower: jasmine (chambeli) · National animal: markhor · National bird: chakor.
  • National poet: Allama Muhammad Iqbal · Founder of the nation: Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
  • The national flag is dark green with a white stripe; the crescent and star stand for progress and light. It was adopted on 11 August 1947.
  • Pakistan Day is celebrated on 23 March (for the 1940 Resolution) and Independence Day on 14 August.
  • The national anthem, “Qaumi Tarana”, was written by Hafeez Jalandhari and its tune was composed by Ahmed G. Chagla.
