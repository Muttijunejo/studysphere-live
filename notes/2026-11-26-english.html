Active and Passive Voice — Rules, Tense Table & Test Traps

English Active & Passive Voice 10 Practice MCQs

In almost every English paper of SPSC and screening tests, two or three questions come from active and passive voice — and they are free marks once you know the one golden rule. These notes explain the rule step by step in simple English with Roman Urdu help, with the full tense table and the traps that tests repeat.

1. What Are Active and Passive Voice?

Active voice: the subject of the sentence does the action. Roman Urdu: Active voice mein subject khud kaam karta hai.

She writes a letter.   (She = doer, writes = action, a letter = object)

Passive voice: the subject of the sentence receives the action. The doer moves to the end, after "by". Roman Urdu: Passive voice mein subject par kaam hota hai — kaam karne wala "by" ke baad aata hai.

A letter is written by her.   (A letter = receiver, is written = action, by her = doer)

Both sentences say the same thing — only the focus changes. In the active sentence we focus on who does the work; in the passive sentence we focus on what the work is done to.

When do we use the passive? (1) When the doer is unknown: "My bicycle was stolen." (we do not know who stole it). (2) When the doer is not important: "The road was repaired." (3) In formal and scientific writing: "The experiment was completed in two hours."

2. The Golden Rule — How to Change Active into Passive

Every active-to-passive change follows the same three steps. Learn them once and you can change any tense.

Object becomes the Subject + be + past participle (V3) + by + Subject becomes the Object

Step 1: take the object of the active sentence and make it the subject of the passive sentence. ("a letter" moves to the front.)

Step 2: use the correct form of "be" for the tense, then the past participle (V3) of the main verb. ("is written" — "is" is the "be" for present simple, "written" is V3 of "write".)

Step 3: put the old subject after "by" at the end. ("by her" — "she" changes to "her" after "by".)

Active: She (subject) + writes (verb) + a letter (object).
Passive: A letter (subject) + is written (be + V3) + by her.

Remember the pronouns: I → by me, he → by him, she → by her, we → by us, they → by them, you → by you, it → by it. Tests often check this small change: "She writes a letter" becomes "A letter is written by her", never "by she".

The tense never changes. A present-simple active sentence becomes a present-simple passive sentence; a past-simple active sentence becomes a past-simple passive sentence. If the tense changes, the answer is wrong — this is the number-one trap.

3. The Full Tense Table

Only the "be" part changes with the tense. The main verb is always V3 in the passive. Memorise this table and most voice questions become one-step questions.

TenseActivePassive
Simple PresentShe writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
Present ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Present PerfectShe has written a letter.A letter has been written by her.
Simple PastShe wrote a letter.A letter was written by her.
Past ContinuousShe was writing a letter.A letter was being written by her.
Past PerfectShe had written a letter.A letter had been written by her.
Simple FutureShe will write a letter.A letter will be written by her.
Future PerfectShe will have written a letter.A letter will have been written by her.
Modal verbsShe can / must / should write a letter.A letter can / must / should be written by her.

Easy way to remember: continuous tenses use "being" (is being, was being), perfect tenses use "been" (has been, had been, will have been), and simple tenses use plain "be" (is, was, will be). Roman Urdu: Continuous mein "being", perfect mein "been", aur simple tense mein sirf "is/was/will be" — ye teen lafz yaad kar lein to table yaad ho jata hai.

Note: present perfect continuous, past perfect continuous and future continuous have no passive voice in normal use — tests never ask them.

4. When the Agent Is Dropped (No "by ...")

In many passive sentences the "by + doer" part is left out — and that is correct. The doer is dropped when:

  • It is unknown: "My pen was stolen." (We do not know who stole it.)
  • It is not important: "The house was built in 1990." (Who built it does not matter.)
  • It is obvious or general: "English is spoken all over the world." (By people in general — no need to say it.)

Test tip: if a passive sentence has no "by" phrase, it is not wrong. Do not add "by someone" or "by them" yourself — the test only asks you to change the voice, not to invent a doer.

5. Questions, Imperatives and Modals

Questions: move the "be" verb to the front, just like in active questions.

Did she write the letter? → Was the letter written by her?
Is he repairing the car? → Is the car being repaired by him?

"Who" questions: "Who" becomes "By whom" in the passive. This is a favourite test question.

Who broke the glass? → By whom was the glass broken?
Who wrote this book? → By whom was this book written?

Imperatives (orders and requests): start the passive with "Let".

Open the window. → Let the window be opened.
Please help me. → Let me be helped.   (or: You are requested to help me.)

Modals: can, could, may, might, must, should and would are followed by "be + V3" in the passive. The modal itself never changes.

You must finish the work. → The work must be finished by you.
He can solve this problem. → This problem can be solved by him.

6. Verbs with Two Objects

Some verbs take two objects — a person and a thing. "Give", "send", "offer", "tell" and "teach" are the common ones. Either object can become the subject of the passive sentence, so there are two correct passives.

She gave me a book.
→ I was given a book by her.   (person becomes subject)
→ A book was given to me by her.   (thing becomes subject)

Test tip: if both forms appear in the options, the one with the person as subject is usually the expected answer. Watch for "to": when the thing becomes the subject, the person keeps "to" — "A book was given to me", never "A book was given me".

7. Verbs with No Passive Voice

A sentence can be made passive only if the verb has an object. Verbs that take no object — called intransitive verbs — have no passive voice at all.

He sleeps.   She arrived.   They laughed.   The baby cried.

None of these can be made passive, because there is no object to move to the front. Roman Urdu: Jin verbs ka object hi nahi hota (sona, aana, hansna), un ki passive voice banti hi nahi — ye sawal test mein "which sentence cannot be changed into passive" ki shakal mein aata hai.

Common intransitive verbs: sleep, arrive, go, come, laugh, cry, die, sit, stand, walk, run, swim, happen. But be careful — some verbs are both: "He runs a shop" (has an object, "a shop") can be passive: "A shop is run by him."

8. Test Traps — Ghaltiyan Jo Tests Mein Repeat Hoti Hain

  • Trap 1 — tense change: "She writes a letter" → "A letter was written by her" is WRONG. The tense must stay present: "is written".
  • Trap 2 — "being" missing: "They are building a house" → "A house is built by them" is WRONG. Present continuous needs "is being built".
  • Trap 3 — "been" missing: "He has finished the work" → "The work is finished by him" is WRONG. Present perfect needs "has been finished".
  • Trap 4 — V3 forgotten: "The letter is write by her" is WRONG. After "be" the verb is always V3: "written", never "write" or "writing".
  • Trap 5 — wrong pronoun: "A letter is written by she" is WRONG. After "by" use the object pronoun: "by her".
  • Trap 6 — "who" questions: "Who broke the glass?" → "By whom was the glass broken?" — "By who" is wrong; use "By whom".
  • Trap 7 — intransitive verbs: "He sleeps" has no passive. If a question asks to change it, the answer is "cannot be changed".

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Golden rule: Object → Subject + be + V3 + by + Subject → Object.
  • The main verb is always V3 in the passive; only the "be" part changes with the tense.
  • Continuous tenses use "being" (is being, was being); perfect tenses use "been" (has been, had been).
  • The tense never changes when active becomes passive.
  • "By + doer" is dropped when the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious.
  • "Who" becomes "By whom" in passive questions.
  • Imperatives start with "Let": "Open the door" → "Let the door be opened."
  • Modals take "be + V3": can be solved, must be finished, should be written.
  • With two objects, either can become the subject: "I was given a book" / "A book was given to me".
  • Intransitive verbs (sleep, arrive, go, laugh) have no passive voice.
  • Present perfect continuous, past perfect continuous and future continuous have no passive.

Practice MCQs — 10 Questions

Try these first, then check the answer key at the end.

  1. Choose the passive voice of: "She writes a letter."
    (a) A letter is written by her.   (b) A letter was written by her.   (c) A letter is being written by her.   (d) A letter has been written by her.
  2. Choose the passive voice of: "They are building a house."
    (a) A house is built by them.   (b) A house is being built by them.   (c) A house was built by them.   (d) A house has been built by them.
  3. Choose the passive voice of: "He has finished the work."
    (a) The work is finished by him.   (b) The work was finished by him.   (c) The work has been finished by him.   (d) The work is being finished by him.
  4. Choose the passive voice of: "She wrote a poem."
    (a) A poem is written by her.   (b) A poem was written by her.   (c) A poem has been written by her.   (d) A poem is being written by her.
  5. Choose the passive voice of: "The boys were playing cricket."
    (a) Cricket is played by the boys.   (b) Cricket was played by the boys.   (c) Cricket was being played by the boys.   (d) Cricket has been played by the boys.
  6. Choose the passive voice of: "I will help you."
    (a) You are helped by me.   (b) You will be helped by me.   (c) You were helped by me.   (d) You have been helped by me.
  7. Choose the passive voice of: "Open the window."
    (a) The window is opened.   (b) Let the window be opened.   (c) The window was opened.   (d) Let the window opened.
  8. Choose the passive voice of: "Who broke the glass?"
    (a) By whom was the glass broken?   (b) Who was the glass broken by?   (c) The glass was broken by who?   (d) By whom the glass was broken?
  9. Choose the passive voice of: "The teacher punished the boy."
    (a) The boy is punished by the teacher.   (b) The boy was punished by the teacher.   (c) The boy has been punished by the teacher.   (d) The boy is being punished by the teacher.
  10. Choose the passive voice of: "Can you solve this problem?"
    (a) Can this problem solved by you?   (b) Can this problem be solve by you?   (c) Can this problem be solved by you?   (d) Could this problem be solved by you?

Answer Key

1 — (a) A letter is written by her.  ·  2 — (b) A house is being built by them.  ·  3 — (c) The work has been finished by him.  ·  4 — (b) A poem was written by her.  ·  5 — (c) Cricket was being played by the boys.  ·  6 — (b) You will be helped by me.  ·  7 — (b) Let the window be opened.  ·  8 — (a) By whom was the glass broken?  ·  9 — (b) The boy was punished by the teacher.  ·  10 — (c) Can this problem be solved by you?
