English Grammar Rules — Wren & Martin Style

Har rule pehle English mein bold diya gaya hai aur us ke neeche asaan Roman Urdu mein halke rang mein samjhaya gaya hai — bilkul Wren & Martin ki tarah: pehle rule, phir example. Poori tafseel English Book (PDF) mein hai.

1. Parts of Speech

  • Noun: a word that names a person, place, thing or idea. Kisi shakhs, jagah ya cheez ka naam. Example: Ali lives in Karachi. Honesty is a great quality.
  • Pronoun: a word used instead of a noun. Naam ki jagah istemal hone wala lafz, taaki naam baar baar na dohrana pare. Example: Sara is happy because she passed the test.
  • Verb: a word that shows an action or a state. Kaam ya haalat batane wala lafz. Example: They play cricket. He is tired.
  • Adjective: a word that describes a noun or pronoun. Naam ki khoobi ya khaami batata hai. Example: It is a difficult question. She is honest.
  • Adverb: a word that describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb. Kaam kab, kahan, kaise hua — ye batata hai. Example: He runs quickly. She came yesterday.
  • Preposition: a word that shows the relation of a noun with another word. Jagah, waqt ya talluq batata hai. Example: The book is on the table. He goes to school.
  • Conjunction: a word that joins words or sentences. Do alfaaz ya jumlon ko jorta hai. Example: Ali and Bilal are friends. I was ill, so I stayed home.
  • Interjection: a word that expresses a sudden feeling. Achanak khushi, gham ya hairat ka izhaar; is ke baad aksar ! lagta hai. Example: Hurrah! We have won the match.

2. Articles — A, An, The

  • Use A before a singular countable noun that begins with a consonant sound. Faisla spelling se nahi, lafz ki pehli AWAAZ se hota hai. Example: I have a pen and a book.
  • Use AN before a singular countable noun that begins with a vowel sound. Jis lafz ki pehli awaaz a, e, i, o, u wali ho, us se pehle an aata hai. Example: She ate an apple and an egg.
  • Use AN before a silent H: an hour, an honest man. Kuch alfaaz mein H khamosh hota hai, awaaz vowel ki aati hai. Example: He is an honest officer.
  • Use A before words that begin with a 'yu' or 'wa' sound: a university, a one-rupee note. University ki pehli awaaz 'yu' consonant jaisi hai, is liye a lagta hai. Example: He studies in a university.
  • Use THE for a particular person or thing already known or already mentioned. Jo cheez sunne wale ko maloom ho ya pehle zikr ho chuki ho. Example: The book on the table is mine.
  • Use THE before unique things, superlatives, rivers, oceans and holy books. Duniya mein akeli cheezain (sun, moon) aur sab se bara darja (best, first). Example: The sun rises in the east.
  • Use NO article before languages, games, subjects and meals in general. Zubaanon, khelon aur mazameen ke naam se pehle a/an/the nahi aata. Example: He speaks English and plays cricket.

4. Active & Passive Voice

  • Passive formula: Object + helping verb (be) + V3 + by + Subject. Active ka object passive ka subject ban jata hai, aur verb hamesha teesri form (V3) mein aati hai.
  • Present Simple: He writes a letter. → A letter is written by him.
  • Past Simple: He wrote a letter. → A letter was written by him.
  • Present Continuous: He is writing a letter. → A letter is being written by him.
  • Future Simple: He will write a letter. → A letter will be written by him.
  • Jab kaam karne wala maloom na ho ya aham na ho, to 'by' wala hissa aksar hata dete hain. Example: The road was repaired last year.

5. Direct & Indirect Speech

  • In indirect speech the tense moves one step back: present simple → past simple, present continuous → past continuous, present perfect → past perfect, will → would, can → could. Reported speech mein tense ek darja peeche chala jata hai — ise backshift kehte hain.
  • Time words also change: today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next day, now → then, here → there.
  • He said, "I am busy." → He said that he was busy.
  • She said to me, "I will help you." → She told me that she would help me. Jis ko baat kahi jaye wo mojood ho to 'said to' ki jagah 'told' aata hai.
  • For questions use if/whether and keep the statement order. Sawalia jumla indirect mein aam jumle ki tarteeb leta hai. Example: He asked me if I was ready.

6. Prepositions

  • AT for exact time and exact points: at 9 o'clock, at the bus stop. Bilkul tay waqt ya chhoti si jagah ke liye at.
  • ON for days, dates and surfaces: on Monday, on 8 November, on the table.
  • IN for months, years, cities and enclosed places: in June, in 2026, in Karachi, in the room. Waqt jitna bara, preposition utna bara: at (point) → on (din) → in (mahina/saal).
  • Fixed pairs — good at, afraid of, interested in, married to, depend on, listen to. Ye joray hamesha sath aate hain, test mein aksar pooche jate hain. Example: He is good at English. Success depends on hard work.

7. Subject–Verb Agreement

  • A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. He/She/It ke sath verb par s/es lagta hai. Example: He plays. They play.
  • Each, every, either, neither and everyone take a singular verb. Ye alfaaz dekhne mein bare lagte hain lekin grammer mein singular hain. Example: Each of the boys has a pen.
  • Two subjects joined by 'and' take a plural verb. Lekin ek hi cheez ka naam ho to singular. Example: Ali and Bilal are friends. Bread and butter is my breakfast.
  • With 'or' and 'nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Verb qareeb wale subject ke mutabiq aata hai. Example: Neither the teacher nor the students were present.
  • Uncountable nouns (information, advice, furniture, news) take a singular verb. In ka plural nahi banta aur sath 'is' aata hai. Example: The information is useful.
  • 'The number of' takes a singular verb; 'a number of' takes a plural verb. Example: The number of students is fifty. A number of students were absent.

Synonyms & Antonyms

A synonym has nearly the same meaning; an antonym has the opposite meaning.

Synonyms: happy – joyful · begin – start · brave – bold · rich – wealthy · fast – quick · big – large · end – finish · clever – smart.

Antonyms: brave – cowardly · ancient – modern · honest – dishonest · victory – defeat · expand – contract · arrival – departure · include – exclude · maximum – minimum.

Test tip: in a synonym question, first find the general sense of the word (positive or negative), then choose the closest option.

8. Common Errors

  • Wrong: He don't like tea. Right: He doesn't like tea. He/she/it ke sath does aata hai.
  • Wrong: She is more cleverer. Right: She is more clever. -er aur more ek sath kabhi nahi aate.
  • Wrong: I have seen him yesterday. Right: I saw him yesterday. Yesterday ke sath past simple aata hai.
  • Wrong: The informations are useful. Right: The information is useful. Information uncountable hai — na plural banta hai, na 'are' aata hai.
  • Wrong: He told that he was busy. Right: He said that he was busy. Tell ke sath object lazmi hai: He told me that…
  • Wrong: She is married with a doctor. Right: She is married to a doctor. Married ke sath hamesha 'to' aata hai.
  • Wrong: I am agree with you. Right: I agree with you. Agree khud verb hai, is se pehle am/is nahi aata.
  • Wrong: He did not went home. Right: He did not go home. Did ke baad hamesha verb ki pehli form (V1) aati hai.

One-Word & Idioms Basics

An idiom is a phrase whose meaning is different from its separate words. Common test idioms:

  • Break the ice — to start a conversation in a social setting.
  • Once in a blue moon — very rarely.
  • A blessing in disguise — something that looks bad but turns out good.
  • To beat about the bush — to avoid saying what one means.
  • One-word basics: a person who teaches is a teacher; a place where birds are kept is an aviary; a life story written by oneself is an autobiography.
