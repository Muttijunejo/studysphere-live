Light — Reflection, Refraction and Lenses

Physics Light & Optics 10 Practice MCQs

One or two questions from light come in almost every physics paper of SPSC and screening tests — mirrors, lenses, refraction and the human eye are all favourite topics. These notes explain the whole chapter in simple English with Roman Urdu help, with the laws, the formulas and the traps that tests repeat.

1. What Is Light?

Light is a form of energy that makes things visible. It travels in a straight line — this is called rectilinear propagation. That is why shadows have a sharp shape: light cannot bend around an object. Roman Urdu: Roshni seedhi lakeer mein chalti hai — isi liye saaye ki shakal saaf hoti hai.

Light is an electromagnetic wave — it needs no medium to travel, so it comes from the Sun to the Earth through empty space. Its speed in a vacuum (and almost the same in air) is 3 × 108 m/s (300,000 km/s) — the fastest thing in the universe. Nothing can travel faster than light.

Speed of light in vacuum/air = 3 × 108 m/s   ·   Speed in glass ≈ 2 × 108 m/s   ·   Speed in water ≈ 2.25 × 108 m/s

Light slows down when it enters a denser medium — glass is denser than water, so light is slowest in glass. Remember the order: air (fastest) > water > glass (slowest).

Visible light is only a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum. Its colours, from longest wavelength to shortest, are: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (VIBGYOR). Red has the longest wavelength (about 700 nm) and violet the shortest (about 400 nm).

2. Reflection — Mirrors

Reflection is the bouncing back of light when it strikes a smooth surface, like a mirror. Roman Urdu: Roshni jab kisi chamkeeli satah se takra kar wapas laut aaye, use reflection kehte hain.

The two laws of reflection:

  • Law 1: The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. (Angle of incidence = angle at which light falls on the mirror; angle of reflection = angle at which it leaves.)
  • Law 2: The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal (the line at 90° to the surface) all lie in the same plane.

Angle of incidence (i) = Angle of reflection (r)

Plane mirror: the image is virtual (it cannot be caught on a screen), erect (upright), the same size as the object, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it. Left and right are reversed — this is called lateral inversion. That is why the word AMBULANCE is written backwards on the front of an ambulance: in a driver's mirror it reads correctly.

Concave mirror (curved inwards, like a bowl): it converges light to a point. Uses: shaving mirrors and make-up mirrors (they give a magnified image), dentists' mirrors, solar cookers and the reflectors of torches and car headlights (to send out a parallel beam).

Convex mirror (curved outwards, like the back of a spoon): it diverges light and always gives a small, erect, virtual image — but it shows a wide area. Uses: rear-view mirrors of vehicles and safety mirrors at blind corners and in shops. Roman Urdu: Convex mirror mein cheez chhoti nazar aati hai, is liye "Objects in the mirror are closer than they appear" likha hota hai.

3. Refraction — Bending of Light

Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium into another — because its speed changes. Roman Urdu: Roshni jab ek medium se doosre medium mein jaye aur us ki speed badal jaye, to woh mur jati hai — isi ko refraction kehte hain.

When light goes from a rarer medium (air) into a denser medium (glass or water), it slows down and bends towards the normal. When it goes from a denser medium back into a rarer one, it speeds up and bends away from the normal.

Refractive index (n) tells how much a medium slows light down:

n = speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed of light in the medium

MediumRefractive index (n)Speed of light
Vacuum / air1.003.00 × 108 m/s
Water1.33 (4/3)2.25 × 108 m/s
Glass1.502.00 × 108 m/s
Diamond2.421.24 × 108 m/s

Everyday examples of refraction: a swimming pool looks shallower than it really is; a pencil in a glass of water looks bent at the surface; the Sun is visible a few minutes before it actually rises and after it sets, because the atmosphere bends its light; stars twinkle because the air bends their light again and again.

4. Total Internal Reflection

Sometimes light does not leave a denser medium at all — it is totally reflected back inside it. This is called total internal reflection (TIR). It happens only when two conditions are both true:

  • Condition 1: light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium (for example, water to air, or glass to air).
  • Condition 2: the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle of that medium. The critical angle of water is about 49° and of glass about 42°.

Uses of TIR: optical fibres — thin glass fibres that carry telephone and internet signals as light, bouncing inside the fibre again and again; mirages in hot deserts — the hot air near the ground bends light so much that the sky looks like water; the sparkle of a diamond — its small critical angle (about 24°) traps light inside, so it shines.

Test tip: if a question says light goes from glass to air at 50°, ask yourself: is 50° more than glass's critical angle 42°? Yes — so the answer is total internal reflection, not refraction.

5. Lenses — Convex and Concave

A lens is a piece of transparent glass with at least one curved surface. There are two kinds:

  • Convex lens (thick in the middle): it converges parallel rays to a point called the focus. It is also called a converging lens. Uses: magnifying glass, camera lens, projector, microscope and telescope — and it corrects long sight.
  • Concave lens (thin in the middle): it diverges parallel rays, as if they came from a point. It is also called a diverging lens. Uses: it corrects short sight, and it is used in some spectacles and peepholes.

Focal length (f) is the distance from the lens to its focus. The power of a lens (P) is how strongly it bends light:

Power of lens (P) = 1 ÷ focal length (f, in metres)   ·   Unit: dioptre (D)

Example: a lens of focal length 0.5 m has power P = 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2 dioptre. A convex lens has positive power (+2 D) and a concave lens has negative power (−2 D). Roman Urdu: Aankh ki taqat (power) isi formula se nikalti hai — focal length jitni chhoti, power utni zyada.

Real vs virtual image: a real image can be caught on a screen (a convex lens makes one when the object is beyond the focus); a virtual image cannot be caught on a screen (a magnifying glass makes a virtual, magnified, erect image when the object is inside the focus).

6. The Human Eye — Defects and Correction

The eye works like a camera: the cornea and lens focus light onto the retina at the back of the eye, and the retina sends the picture to the brain. Two common defects come in tests:

DefectWhat happensCorrected by
Short sight (Myopia)The person sees near objects clearly but not far ones. The image forms in front of the retina (eyeball too long, or lens too strong).Concave lens (diverging — pushes the image back onto the retina).
Long sight (Hypermetropia)The person sees far objects clearly but not near ones. The image forms behind the retina (eyeball too short, or lens too weak).Convex lens (converging — pulls the image forward onto the retina).

Easy way to remember: short sight = conCAVE lens (both have "cave" — the lens is hollowed in the middle). Long sight = conVEX lens. Tests ask this pair again and again, so learn it as one line: "Myopia — concave; Hypermetropia — convex."

Other eye facts: the iris controls how much light enters the eye (it is the coloured part); the pupil is the opening in the middle; the retina has light-sensitive cells — rods (for dim light) and cones (for colour). The least distance of distinct vision for a normal eye is 25 cm.

7. Dispersion — The Rainbow

Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its seven colours when it passes through a glass prism. Roman Urdu: Safed roshni jab prism se guzre to saat rangon mein bat jati hai — isi ko dispersion kehte hain.

White light is a mixture of seven colours. Each colour has a different wavelength, so each bends by a different amount in the prism: violet bends the most and red bends the least. The spectrum on a screen is always in this order: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (VIBGYOR).

The rainbow is nature's dispersion: sunlight is dispersed by tiny raindrops in the air after rain, and the same seven colours appear in the sky. The Sun must be behind you and the rain in front of you for a rainbow to appear.

Primary colours of light are red, green and blue — mixing them in different amounts makes all the other colours on a screen. (Do not confuse them with the primary colours of paint, which are red, blue and yellow.)

8. Test Traps — Ghaltiyan Jo Tests Mein Repeat Hoti Hain

  • Trap 1 — "speed of light" vs "speed of sound": light = 3 × 108 m/s; sound in air = only about 343 m/s. Light is nearly a million times faster — that is why we see lightning before we hear thunder.
  • Trap 2 — mirror or lens: a concave mirror converges light, but a concave lens diverges it. Read the word "mirror" or "lens" first — it flips the answer.
  • Trap 3 — refraction direction: air → glass bends towards the normal; glass → air bends away from the normal. The speed decides the direction: slower = towards, faster = away.
  • Trap 4 — TIR conditions: total internal reflection needs both conditions — denser to rarer and angle greater than the critical angle. One condition alone is not enough.
  • Trap 5 — myopia vs hypermetropia: myopia = concave lens, hypermetropia = convex lens. The two are swapped in the wrong options on purpose.
  • Trap 6 — power of lens: P = 1/f only when f is in metres. A focal length of 50 cm is 0.5 m, so P = 2 D — not 1/50. Convert cm to m first.
  • Trap 7 — "real" vs "virtual": a plane mirror's image is virtual — it looks behind the mirror but nothing is really there, so it can never be caught on a screen.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Light travels in a straight line (rectilinear propagation) at 3 × 108 m/s in vacuum/air.
  • Speed order: air (fastest) > water > glass (slowest).
  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • Plane mirror image: virtual, erect, same size, image distance = object distance, left-right reversed.
  • Concave mirror converges (shaving mirror, torch reflector); convex mirror diverges (rear-view mirror, wide view).
  • Refractive index: air 1.00, water 1.33 (4/3), glass 1.50, diamond 2.42.
  • TIR: denser → rarer medium, angle > critical angle (water 49°, glass 42°). Uses: optical fibre, mirage, diamond sparkle.
  • Convex lens = converging (+ power); concave lens = diverging (− power). Power P = 1/f (f in metres), unit dioptre.
  • Myopia (short sight) = concave lens; Hypermetropia (long sight) = convex lens.
  • Dispersion: white light → 7 colours (VIBGYOR); violet bends most, red bends least.
  • Least distance of distinct vision = 25 cm; primary colours of light = red, green, blue.
