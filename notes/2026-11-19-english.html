English Tenses — Present, Past & Future Made Easy

English Tenses 10 Practice MCQs

Almost every English paper in a screening test asks about tenses — which form of the verb to use, and when. These notes explain, in simple English, the three times (present, past, future) and the four forms (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous), with signal words, the since/for rule, and the traps that cost marks.

What Is a Tense? — Pehle Ye Samjhein

A tense shows the time of an action — when it happens. Roman Urdu: Tense batata hai ke kaam kab hota hai — abhi, pehle, ya baad mein.

There are three times — present (now), past (before now) and future (after now) — and four forms in each time: simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous. That makes 12 tenses in total, but screening tests mostly ask about the nine in the table below.

TenseFormExample
Simple Presentverb / verb + s, esShe teaches English.
Present Continuousam / is / are + verb-ingShe is teaching now.
Present Perfecthas / have + past participleShe has taught for five years.
Simple Pastverb-ed / irregular formShe taught yesterday.
Past Continuouswas / were + verb-ingShe was teaching at 9 o'clock.
Past Perfecthad + past participleShe had taught before he came.
Simple Futurewill + base verbShe will teach tomorrow.
Future Continuouswill be + verb-ingShe will be teaching at 10 o'clock.
Future Perfectwill have + past participleShe will have taught by June.

Test trick: first find the time word in the sentence (yesterday, now, tomorrow, since, already), then choose the tense that matches it. The time word is the biggest clue.

Simple Present — Habits and Facts

Use it for habits, routines and facts that are always true.

he / she / it + verb-s, es  ·  I / you / we / they + base verb

Examples: She goes to school every day. The sun rises in the east. Water boils at 100°C. They play cricket on Sunday.

Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, every week, daily. Roman Urdu: Rozana ki aadat ya hamesha sach baat ke liye simple present use hota hai.

Watch the third person: he/she/it takes -s or -es — he works, she teaches, it rains. Forgetting the -s is the most common mistake in this tense.

Present Continuous — Happening Now

Use it for an action that is happening right now, or a plan for the near future.

am / is / are + verb-ing

Examples: Look! It is raining. I am reading a book now. They are playing in the park. We are going to Lahore tomorrow.

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, Look!, Listen!, today (for a plan). Roman Urdu: Jo kaam abhi ho raha hai, us ke liye present continuous.

Test trap: verbs of feeling and knowing (know, like, love, want, believe, understand) are not used in the continuous form. Say "I know him", not "I am knowing him".

Present Perfect — Just Finished, or Still True

Use it for an action that finished just now, or that started in the past and is still true.

has / have + past participle (verb 3rd form)

Examples: I have just finished my homework. She has lived here since 2015. They have visited Karachi twice.

Signal words: just, already, yet, ever, never, since, for. Roman Urdu: Kaam abhi-abhi khatam hua ho, ya pehle se shuru ho kar abhi tak chal raha ho.

Test trap: never use present perfect with a finished past time word like yesterday, last year or ago. Say "I saw him yesterday" (simple past), not "I have seen him yesterday".

Simple Past — Finished Past Action

Use it for an action that started and finished in the past.

verb-ed (regular)  ·  irregular form (go → went, see → saw, speak → spoke)

Examples: He lived in Lahore last year. She went to school yesterday. We played a match on Sunday.

Signal words: yesterday, last night, last week, ago, in 2010. Roman Urdu: Jo kaam guzre hue waqt mein khatam ho gaya, us ke liye simple past.

Test trap: after "did" the verb stays in its base form — "Did you go?" not "Did you went?" The past is already in "did".

Past Continuous — Action in Progress in the Past

Use it for an action that was in progress at a past time, often when another shorter action happened.

was / were + verb-ing

Examples: While I was reading, my brother was watching TV. She was cooking when the phone rang. At 9 o'clock last night, we were studying.

Signal words: while, when, at that time, all day yesterday. Roman Urdu: Guzre waqt mein jo kaam chal raha tha, us ke liye past continuous — aksar "while" ya "when" ke saath.

Test trick: "while" + long action (continuous), "when" + short action (simple past): "While I was walking, I saw a snake."

Past Perfect — The Earlier of Two Past Actions

Use it when two past actions happened, and you want to show which one came first.

had + past participle (verb 3rd form)

Examples: The train had left before we reached the station. We had eaten our dinner before he came. She had finished her work when I called.

Signal words: before, after, by the time, already. Roman Urdu: Do guzre hue kaamon mein jo pehle hua, woh past perfect mein — baad wala simple past mein.

Test trap: the first (earlier) action takes "had", the second takes simple past — "The train had left before we reached."

Simple Future — What Will Happen

Use it for an action that will happen later.

will + base verb  ·  also: am / is / are + going to + base verb

Examples: They will visit Karachi tomorrow. I will help you. We are going to start a new lesson next week.

Signal words: tomorrow, next week, next month, soon, in the future. Roman Urdu: Aane wale waqt ke kaam ke liye simple future — "will" ke baad verb ki pehli form aati hai.

Test trap: after "will" the verb is always in its base form — "She will go", never "She will goes" or "She will went".

Future Continuous & Future Perfect — A Quick Look

Future Continuous (will be + verb-ing): an action in progress at a future time — "At 10 o'clock tomorrow, she will be teaching."

Future Perfect (will have + past participle): an action that will be finished before a future time — "By next month, I will have finished this book."

Present Perfect Continuous (has/have been + verb-ing): an action that started in the past and is still going on — "She has been learning English for five years."

These three appear less often in screening tests than the six tenses above, but one question on them is common — remember their forms.

Since vs For — The Rule Tests Love

Since + a point of time (when it started): since 2015, since Monday, since morning, since I was a child.

For + a period of time (how long): for five years, for two hours, for a long time, for a week.

Examples: She has lived here since 2015. She has lived here for eleven years. I have been waiting since 9 o'clock — I have been waiting for two hours.

Test trap: "since five years" is wrong — five years is a period, so it needs "for". "For 2015" is wrong — 2015 is a point, so it needs "since".

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • There are 3 times (present, past, future) and 12 tenses in total.
  • Simple Present: verb / verb + s, es — he works, she teaches; signal words: always, usually, every day.
  • Present Continuous: am / is / are + verb-ing — signal words: now, at the moment, Look!
  • Present Perfect: has / have + past participle — signal words: just, already, yet, since, for.
  • Simple Past: verb-ed / irregular — signal words: yesterday, last night, ago.
  • Past Continuous: was / were + verb-ing — used with "while" for the longer action.
  • Past Perfect: had + past participle — the earlier of two past actions.
  • Simple Future: will + base verb — after "will", always the base form of the verb.
  • Since + point of time (since 2015); for + period of time (for five years).
  • Present perfect is never used with yesterday, last year or ago — use simple past.
  • After "did", the verb stays in its base form: "Did you go?"
  • Know, like, love, want, believe and understand are not used in the continuous form.

Practice MCQs — 10 Questions

  1. My father ___ in a bank.
    (a) work   (b) works   (c) working   (d) worked
  2. Look! It ___ raining.
    (a) is   (b) are   (c) was   (d) has
  3. I have ___ finished my homework.
    (a) yet   (b) just   (c) ago   (d) yesterday
  4. He ___ in Lahore last year.
    (a) lives   (b) lived   (c) living   (d) live
  5. While I was reading, my brother ___ TV.
    (a) watches   (b) watched   (c) was watching   (d) is watching
  6. The train had left before we ___ the station.
    (a) reach   (b) reached   (c) reaching   (d) will reach
  7. They ___ Karachi tomorrow.
    (a) visit   (b) visited   (c) will visit   (d) have visited
  8. She ___ English for five years.
    (a) learns   (b) is learning   (c) has been learning   (d) learned
  9. We ___ our dinner before he came.
    (a) eat   (b) ate   (c) had eaten   (d) have eaten
  10. By next month, I ___ this book.
    (a) will finish   (b) will have finished   (c) finish   (d) finished
