General Science Book — Notes & MCQs

General Science · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

This book gathers the everyday science facts that appear in almost every screening test: the human body, basic physics units, simple chemistry and biology. The notes are short and factual, written so you can revise the whole book in one sitting and then test yourself.

Human Body Basics

  • The normal temperature of a healthy human body is about 37 °C (98.6 °F).
  • The heart pumps blood; an adult heart beats about 72 times per minute at rest.
  • The largest organ of the body is the skin. The largest internal organ is the liver.
  • Red blood cells carry oxygen; white blood cells fight germs; platelets help the blood to clot.
  • An adult human has 206 bones. The smallest bone is in the ear and the longest is the thigh bone (femur).
  • The brain controls the body through the nervous system; the kidneys clean the blood and make urine.

Physics — Units, Motion & Energy

  • SI unit of force is the newton (N); of length, the metre; of mass, the kilogram; of electric current, the ampere; of temperature, the kelvin.
  • Speed = distance ÷ time. A body that changes its speed is accelerating.
  • The speed of light in a vacuum is about 300,000 kilometres per second.
  • Energy is the ability to do work; its unit is the joule. Moving energy is kinetic energy; stored energy is potential energy.
  • Sound travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases. It cannot travel in a vacuum.

Chemistry — Atoms, Elements, Acids & Bases

  • An atom has protons and neutrons in the centre (nucleus) and electrons moving around it. The number of protons is the atomic number; carbon has atomic number 6.
  • Common symbols: gold Au, silver Ag, iron Fe, sodium Na, potassium K, water H₂O, carbon dioxide CO₂.
  • Acids taste sour and turn blue litmus red; their pH is less than 7. Bases (alkalis) feel soapy, turn red litmus blue, and have pH more than 7. Pure water is neutral with pH 7.
  • Common salt is sodium chloride (NaCl). The gas we breathe to live is oxygen; the most abundant gas in air is nitrogen (about 78%).

Biology — Cells & Photosynthesis

  • The cell is the smallest unit of life. The mitochondria are called the powerhouse of the cell because they release energy.
  • Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their food. They take in carbon dioxide and water and, using sunlight and the green pigment chlorophyll, produce glucose and release oxygen.
  • Plants take water from the soil through roots; the food made in leaves travels to other parts through vessels.
  • Vitamin D is made in the skin with the help of sunlight and keeps bones strong.

Everyday Science Facts

  • Water boils at 100 °C at sea level and freezes at 0 °C.
  • Mars is called the Red Planet. The planet nearest the Sun is Mercury; the largest planet is Jupiter.
  • A refrigerator keeps food cold by removing heat from inside; a black surface absorbs heat faster than a shiny one.
  • The Earth completes one rotation in about 24 hours, which gives us day and night, and one revolution around the Sun in about 365¼ days.
  • Lightning is a discharge of electricity between clouds, or between a cloud and the ground.
