Physics — Work, Energy and Power

Physics Work, Energy & Power 10 Practice MCQs

What work really means in physics, kinetic and potential energy, the law of conservation of energy, power and the kilowatt-hour — short, simple notes in easy English with Roman Urdu explanations, then 10 practice MCQs with an answer key.

What Is Work? — Kaam

  • Work is done when a force moves an object in the direction of the force. Formula: W = F × d (force × distance moved). Roman Urdu: kaam tab hota hai jab quwat lagane se cheez hile — sirf zor lagana kaafi nahi.
  • The SI unit of work is the joule (J). 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre (1 J = 1 N·m). Work is a scalar quantity — it has magnitude but no direction.
  • Work is ZERO in two cases: (1) no displacement — pushing a wall that does not move; (2) force is perpendicular to the motion — carrying a bag while walking on a flat road (the weight acts down, motion is sideways).
  • If the force and the displacement are in opposite directions (for example friction slowing a moving body), the work is negative.

Energy — Tawanai

  • Energy is the capacity to do work. Its SI unit is also the joule (J). Roman Urdu: tawanai woh salahiyat hai jis se kaam kiya ja sakay.
  • Kinetic energy (KE) — energy of a moving body: KE = ½mv² (m = mass, v = velocity). If the speed doubles, KE becomes four times — because v is squared.
  • Potential energy (PE) — stored energy because of position: PE = mgh (m = mass, g = 9.8 m/s², h = height). Water stored in a dam, a stretched spring and a raised hammer all have potential energy.
  • Other forms of energy: heat, light, sound, electrical, chemical (food, fuel, batteries) and nuclear energy.

Law of Conservation of Energy

  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed — it can only be changed from one form to another. This is the law of conservation of energy, one of the most repeated test questions.
  • Examples of energy change: electric fan — electrical energy → kinetic energy; electric bulb — electrical energy → light + heat; a falling stone — potential energy → kinetic energy; a dam — potential energy of water → kinetic energy → electrical energy.
  • The total energy of an isolated system always stays the same — only its form changes.

Power — Taaqat

  • Power is the rate of doing work: P = W / t (work ÷ time). Roman Urdu: taaqat batati hai ke kaam kitni jaldi hua.
  • The SI unit of power is the watt (W). 1 watt = 1 joule per second. 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W. 1 horsepower (hp) = 746 W.
  • Kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit of ENERGY used in electricity bills — 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. It is a unit of energy, NOT of power — a favourite test trap.
  • Efficiency = (useful output energy ÷ input energy) × 100%. No machine is 100% efficient — some energy is always lost as heat or sound.

Common Test Traps

  • Joule is the unit of work and energy; watt is the unit of power — do not mix them.
  • Work needs displacement: pushing a wall with great force but no movement = zero work.
  • Doubling the speed makes KE four times, not double (KE = ½mv²).
  • kWh is energy (electricity bill unit); kW is power. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  • A body moving with uniform velocity has zero acceleration — but it still has kinetic energy.

Practice MCQs — Work, Energy and Power (Q1–Q10)

  1. One joule is equal to:
    (a) One newton-metre   (b) One watt   (c) One kilogram-metre per second   (d) One newton per second
  2. A 2 kg object moves with a velocity of 3 m/s. Its kinetic energy is:
    (a) 6 J   (b) 9 J   (c) 12 J   (d) 18 J
  3. A 5 kg object is raised to a height of 2 m (g = 10 m/s²). Its potential energy is:
    (a) 10 J   (b) 50 J   (c) 100 J   (d) 200 J
  4. If 100 J of work is done in 5 seconds, the power is:
    (a) 5 W   (b) 10 W   (c) 20 W   (d) 500 W
  5. One kilowatt-hour is equal to:
    (a) 3.6 × 10³ J   (b) 3.6 × 10⁵ J   (c) 3.6 × 10⁶ J   (d) 3.6 × 10⁷ J
  6. Which of the following has the greatest kinetic energy?
    (a) A bicycle moving at 10 km/h   (b) A car moving at 60 km/h   (c) A ball lying at rest   (d) A book placed on a shelf
  7. If a force is applied to a wall but the wall does not move, the work done is:
    (a) Zero   (b) Force × distance   (c) Force ÷ distance   (d) Infinite
  8. One horsepower is equal to:
    (a) 100 W   (b) 550 W   (c) 746 W   (d) 1000 W
  9. The energy stored in a stretched spring or in water stored in a dam is:
    (a) Kinetic energy   (b) Potential energy   (c) Heat energy   (d) Sound energy
  10. If the speed of a moving body is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes:
    (a) Double   (b) Four times   (c) Half   (d) The same
