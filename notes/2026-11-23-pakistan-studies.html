The Constitutions of Pakistan — 1956, 1962 and 1973

Pakistan Studies Constitutions 10 Practice MCQs

Pakistan has had three constitutions — 1956, 1962 and 1973. Job tests ask about all three: when each one came, what kind of government it gave, and how it ended. These notes put the three side by side in simple English with Roman Urdu help, so you can answer any of these questions with confidence.

1. Why Did Pakistan Have Three Constitutions?

When Pakistan came into being on 14 August 1947, it had no constitution of its own. The country was run under the Government of India Act 1935, with some changes, for almost nine years. The first Constituent Assembly took a long time to agree on a constitution — that is why the first one came only in 1956. Roman Urdu: Pakistan banne ke baad taqreeban nau saal tak apna aain nahi tha — mulk 1935 ke Act ke tehet chalaya gaya. Pehla aain 1956 mein bana.

The Objectives Resolution, passed on 12 March 1949, was the base of every constitution that followed. It said that Pakistan would be a state where Muslims could live by Islam, and where minorities would have their rights protected. The Objectives Resolution later became the preamble of the 1973 Constitution.

Remember the pattern: a constitution is made, then it is abrogated (khatam kar diya jata hai) by martial law, then a new one is made. 1956 ended in 1958, 1962 ended in 1969, and 1973 is still the constitution of Pakistan today — it was suspended in 1977 but restored, and changed many times by amendments.

3. The 1956 Constitution — The First One

  • Passed by the Constituent Assembly on 29 February 1956 and enforced on 23 March 1956. On that day Pakistan became a Republic — that is why 23 March is celebrated as Pakistan Day.
  • It was a parliamentary constitution with 234 articles. The President was the head of state, and the Prime Minister ran the government.
  • It declared Pakistan an Islamic Republic and gave fundamental rights to the citizens.
  • It was abrogated on 7 October 1958, when the first martial law was declared. General Ayub Khan became the Chief Martial Law Administrator, and later President.

Test trap: do not mix up the two dates — the constitution was passed on 29 February 1956 but enforced on 23 March 1956. Tests ask both dates separately.

4. The 1962 Constitution — The Presidential One

  • Enforced on 8 June 1962 by President Ayub Khan. It had 250 articles.
  • It introduced a presidential form of government — the President was both head of state and head of government. There was no Prime Minister under this constitution.
  • It was based on the Basic Democracies system, introduced by Ayub Khan in 1959. Under this system, local councillors (Basic Democrats) elected the President.
  • It was abrogated on 25 March 1969, when General Yahya Khan imposed martial law.

Remember: 1962 = presidential + Basic Democracies + 250 articles. Ayub Khan's name comes with this constitution in every test.

5. The 1973 Constitution — The One Still in Force

  • Passed unanimously by the National Assembly on 10 April 1973 and enforced on 14 August 1973. It was framed by a committee headed by Abdul Hafeez Pirzada.
  • It is a parliamentary constitution with 280 articles at the start. The Prime Minister is the head of government; the President is the head of state.
  • Article 2 declares Islam the state religion of Pakistan. The Objectives Resolution is its preamble.
  • The Senate (upper house of Parliament) was created under this constitution. Its first Chairman was Habibullah Khan.
  • The first President under the 1973 Constitution was Fazal Ilahi Chaudhry (1973–1978).
  • It was suspended on 5 July 1977 by General Zia-ul-Haq, and restored later with amendments. The 8th Amendment (1985) gave the President the power to dissolve the National Assembly — Article 58(2)(b).
  • The 18th Amendment (2010) gave more powers to the provinces, removed the President's power to dissolve the Assembly, and gave the provinces control over many subjects.

Test trick: if a question asks for the article number of Islam as state religion, the answer is Article 2. If it asks when the 1973 Constitution came into force, the answer is 14 August 1973.

6. Before the Constitutions — Two Orders You Must Know

  • Legal Framework Order (LFO) 1970 — issued by General Yahya Khan. Under the LFO, the general elections of 1970 were held — the first general elections of Pakistan.
  • Radcliffe Award — announced on 17 August 1947. It fixed the boundaries between Pakistan and India. It was drawn up by Sir Cyril Radcliffe.
  • Objectives Resolution — passed 12 March 1949, presented by Liaquat Ali Khan. It is the base of all three constitutions and the preamble of the 1973 Constitution.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Three constitutions: 1956 (234 articles, parliamentary), 1962 (250 articles, presidential), 1973 (280 articles, parliamentary).
  • Pakistan became a Republic on 23 March 1956. The 1956 Constitution was passed on 29 February 1956.
  • First martial law: 7 October 1958 — Ayub Khan became Chief Martial Law Administrator; the 1956 Constitution was abrogated.
  • 1962 Constitution = presidential system + Basic Democracies (1959); abrogated 25 March 1969 by Yahya Khan.
  • 1973 Constitution: passed 10 April 1973, enforced 14 August 1973, framed by the committee of Abdul Hafeez Pirzada.
  • Article 2 = Islam is the state religion. The Senate was created under the 1973 Constitution.
  • First President under the 1973 Constitution: Fazal Ilahi Chaudhry. First Chairman of the Senate: Habibullah Khan.
  • 1973 Constitution suspended 5 July 1977 by Zia-ul-Haq. 8th Amendment (1985) = President's power to dissolve the Assembly. 18th Amendment (2010) = more powers to the provinces.
  • LFO 1970 was issued by Yahya Khan. Radcliffe Award was announced on 17 August 1947.
