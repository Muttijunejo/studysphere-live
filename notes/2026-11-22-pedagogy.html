Assessment and Evaluation in Education — Types, Validity and Reliability

Every job test for teachers asks about assessment and evaluation — what the two words mean, the four types of assessment, and what makes a test good. These notes explain the whole topic in simple English with Roman Urdu help, so you can answer these questions with confidence.

1. Three Words Students Mix Up — Measurement, Assessment, Evaluation

These three words look alike, but tests ask them separately. Learn the difference once and the questions become easy. Roman Urdu: Ye teeno lafz aik jese lagte hain, lekin test mein in ka farq poocha jata hai — farq aik baar samajh lo to sawal asaan ho jate hain.

WordWhat it meansExample
MeasurementGiving a number or score to a student's workAli got 18 marks out of 20 in the test.
AssessmentCollecting information about how much a student has learned (tests, homework, observation)The teacher gives a quiz, checks homework and watches class work.
EvaluationMaking a judgement or decision from that informationOn the basis of the marks, the teacher decides Ali should be promoted.

Remember it this way: measurement gives a number, assessment gathers the evidence, evaluation makes the decision. Evaluation is the widest word — it includes measurement and assessment inside it.

Test trap: if a question asks "which gives marks in numbers", the answer is measurement, not evaluation. If it asks "which makes a judgement", the answer is evaluation.

2. Purposes of Assessment — Why Do Teachers Assess?

  • To find out how much students have learned and where they are weak.
  • To give feedback to students so they can improve.
  • To help the teacher improve his own teaching — if most students fail one question, the teaching of that topic was weak.
  • To motivate students — a test makes students revise and work hard.
  • To make decisions — promotion, selection, grouping and certification.
  • To diagnose learning difficulties and plan remedial teaching.

3. Four Types of Assessment — The Heart of This Topic

Four types come in almost every pedagogy paper. Learn them by WHEN each one happens. Roman Urdu: Ye chaar qismein har pedagogy paper mein aati hain — in ko WHEN (kab hoti hai) se yaad rakho.

TypeWhen it happensPurposeExample
Formative assessmentDuring teaching, while the course is going onTo improve learning and teaching; gives feedbackA short quiz after every lesson; classwork and homework checks
Summative assessmentAt the end of a course or termTo judge the final result; grading, promotion, certificationFinal examination; annual exam; end-of-term test
Diagnostic assessmentBefore teaching, or when a problem appearsTo find the cause of a learning difficultyA test to find why a student is weak in fractions
Placement assessmentBefore admission or a new courseTo place a student in the right class or groupAn entry test that puts a student in the right section

Test trick: formative = during (formation of learning), summative = summary at the end, diagnostic = finds the disease (difficulty), placement = places the student. If a question says "assessment during teaching", the answer is formative; "at the end of the course", summative.

Do not mix up diagnostic and placement: both happen before teaching, but diagnostic finds the cause of a weakness while placement finds the right class for the student.

4. Norm-Referenced vs Criterion-Referenced Tests

TypeHow a student is judgedExample
Norm-referenced testThe student's score is compared with other students (the "norm" = the group)A test where Ali's marks are compared with the class average; position in the class
Criterion-referenced testThe student's score is compared with a fixed standard (the "criterion")A test where 33% marks are needed to pass, whoever the other students are

Remember: norm = group, criterion = fixed standard. Entry tests and competitive exams are usually norm-referenced; a driving test or a pass/fail school exam is criterion-referenced.

5. Types of Test Items — Objective and Subjective

Objective-type items have one fixed correct answer — any examiner gives the same marks. Examples: multiple-choice questions (MCQs), true/false, matching pairs, fill in the blanks. Roman Urdu: Objective sawal ka aik hi sahi jawab hota hai — koi bhi teacher check kare, number wohi milenge.

Subjective-type items have no single fixed answer — the examiner's judgement matters. Examples: essay questions and short-answer questions. An essay question mainly tests written expression and organisation of ideas, not only memory.

Short-answer questions are best for testing recall — the student must remember the answer without options being given.

Speed test vs power test: a speed test has a fixed time limit and tests how fast a student can work (many easy questions, little time). A power test has no strict time limit and tests how difficult a problem a student can solve — its questions are usually arranged from easy to difficult.

Oral test — the student answers by speaking (viva). Practical test — the student performs a task, such as a science experiment.

6. Four Qualities of a Good Test

A good test must have four qualities. Tests ask their definitions again and again. Roman Urdu: Aik ache test ki chaar khoobiyan hoti hain — in ki tareefain test mein baar baar aati hain.

QualityWhat it meansExample
ValidityThe test measures what it claims to measureA maths test must test maths, not English reading
ReliabilityThe test gives the same result again and againThe same student gets nearly the same marks if he takes the test twice
ObjectivityDifferent examiners give the same marks to the same answerMCQs are objective; essay marking can be less objective
Practicability (usability)The test is easy to use, cheap and takes little timeA test that any teacher can conduct in one period

Test trick: validity is the most important quality — a test that does not measure what it should measure is useless, even if it is reliable. Remember the pair: validity = right thing, reliability = same result, objectivity = same marks by any examiner, practicability = easy and cheap.

Test trap: "subjectivity" is not a quality of a good test — it is a weakness. If a question asks which is NOT a quality of a good test, and subjectivity is in the options, that is the answer.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Measurement gives a number; assessment gathers evidence; evaluation makes a judgement or decision. Evaluation is the widest term.
  • Formative assessment happens during teaching; summative at the end of a course; diagnostic finds the cause of a difficulty; placement puts a student in the right class.
  • Norm-referenced = compared with the group; criterion-referenced = compared with a fixed standard (for example 33% to pass).
  • Objective items: MCQ, true/false, matching, fill in the blanks. Subjective items: essay and short-answer questions.
  • An essay question mainly tests written expression and organisation of ideas; a short-answer question tests recall without options.
  • Speed test = fixed time limit, tests speed. Power test = no strict time limit, questions arranged easy to difficult.
  • Four qualities of a good test: validity (measures what it claims), reliability (same result again), objectivity (same marks by any examiner), practicability (easy, cheap, quick).
  • Validity is the most important quality of a test. Subjectivity is NOT a quality of a good test.
  • Assessment gives feedback to students and helps the teacher improve teaching.

  1. Different examiners mark the same answer paper and give the same marks. Which quality of the test is this?
    (a) Validity   (b) Reliability   (c) Objectivity   (d) Practicability
  2. A test is easy to use, cheap and takes little time. It has the quality of…
    (a) Validity   (b) Practicability   (c) Reliability   (d) Objectivity
  3. Which type of test has a fixed time limit and mainly tests how fast a student can work?
    (a) Power test   (b) Speed test   (c) Diagnostic test   (d) Oral test
  4. An essay-type test mainly measures a student’s…
    (a) Speed of writing   (b) Written expression and organisation of ideas   (c) Memory of dates   (d) Drawing skill
  5. Which of the following is an objective-type test item?
    (a) Essay question   (b) Short-answer question   (c) Multiple-choice question   (d) Oral question
  6. Which assessment is used to place a student in the right class or group?
    (a) Formative assessment   (b) Summative assessment   (c) Placement assessment   (d) Diagnostic assessment
  7. What is the main difference between measurement and evaluation?
    (a) Measurement gives numbers, evaluation gives a judgement about them   (b) Measurement is only for maths   (c) Evaluation gives numbers, measurement gives a judgement   (d) There is no difference
  8. In a power test, the questions are usually…
    (a) All equally difficult   (b) Arranged from easy to difficult, with no strict time limit   (c) Only true/false   (d) Asked orally
  9. Which of the following is NOT a quality of a good test?
    (a) Validity   (b) Reliability   (c) Subjectivity   (d) Objectivity
  10. Short-answer questions are best for testing…
    (a) Guessing   (b) Recall without giving options   (c) Speed only   (d) Handwriting only
