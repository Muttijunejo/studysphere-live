Urdu & Sindhi Book — Grammar Notes & MCQs

Urdu & Sindhi · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

Sindh teaching tests (PST, JEST) include Urdu and Sindhi language questions, and general tests often ask about Urdu grammar, idioms and the great poets of Sindh. This book explains the basic grammar in simple Roman Urdu and English, gives famous muhavare with meanings, and shares key facts about Sindhi language and Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai — with 15 practice MCQs at the end.

Urdu Grammar Basics — Ism, Fail, Harf

Urdu grammar mein har lafz (word) ko teen bare hisson mein baanta gaya hai:

  • Ism (Noun) — kisi shakhs, jagah, cheez ya khayal ka naam. Examples: Ali, Karachi, kitaab, mohabbat. Agar lafz kisi ka naam hai, woh ism hai.
  • Fail (Verb) — woh lafz jo kisi kaam ka karna ya hona bataye, aksar zamane (time) ke saath. Examples: khaana, parhna, sona, jaana. Sentence "Ali kitaab parhta hai" mein parhta hai fail hai.
  • Harf (Particle) — woh lafz jo ism aur fail ko aapas mein jode ya un ke matlab mein tabdeeli laye, lekin akela poora matlab na de. Examples: ne, ko, se, mein, par, ka, ki, aur, lekin.

Kuch aur aham points: Jumla (sentence) alfaz ka woh majmua hai jo poora matlab de. Muzakkar/Muannas Urdu mein gender ko kehte hain — misaal ladka muzakkar, ladki muannas. Woh lafz jo ism ki khoobi ya khaami bataye use Sifat (adjective) kehte hain, jese acha, bara, purana. Zameer (pronoun) ism ki jagah istemal hota hai, jese main, tum, woh, hum.

Urdu Muhavare (Idioms) — Short List

Muhavara woh jumla ya alfaaz hain jo apne asal matlab ke bajaye khaas matlab mein istemal hon. Tests mein in ke meanings bohat poochhe jate hain:

  • Aam ke aam, guthliyon ke daam — dohra faida uthana (double benefit).
  • Naach na jaane, aangan tedha — apni ghalti doosron par daalna (blaming others for one's own fault).
  • Nau do gyarah hona — bhaag jaana / rafu chakkar hona (to run away).
  • Eent ka jawaab patthar se dena — sakht jawaab dena (a strong reply).
  • Haathon ke tote udna — hairan pareshan ho jaana (to be stunned).
  • Daal mein kuch kaala hona — kuch garbar ya shak hona (something suspicious).
  • Aankh ka taara — bohat pyaara (very dear).
  • Khayaali pulao pakana — khush fehmi mein rehna, na-mumkin khwab dekhna (daydreaming).

Sindh and the Sindhi Language — Key Facts

  • Sindhi is one of the oldest languages of the Indus Valley and is the provincial language of Sindh. It is also spoken in parts of Balochistan, Punjab and by Sindhi communities in India and around the world.
  • In Pakistan, Sindhi is written in a modified Arabic-based script with 52 letters. In India, the Devanagari script is also used for Sindhi.
  • The present Sindhi script was officially standardised in the 19th century (1853) during British rule.
  • Sindhi has official status in Sindh; Sindhi newspapers, books and TV channels keep the language alive, and the language of the ancient city of Mohenjo-daro shows how old the culture of this land is.
  • Great classical poets of Sindhi include Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Sachal Sarmast and Sami. The modern national poet of Sindh is often considered Shaikh Ayaz.

Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai — Basics

  • Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai (1689–1752) is the greatest Sufi poet of Sindhi language. He was born in Hala Haveli (near Hala, Sindh) and his shrine is at Bhit Shah, where his Urs is held every year.
  • His famous poetry book is called Shah Jo Risalo. It is a collection of chapters called Sur, each linked with music and a folk story.
  • The Risalo is famous for its seven heroines (Sat Surayoon) — Marvi, Moomal, Sassui, Sohni, Laila, Noori and Sorath — who stand for courage, love, patience and the search for truth.
  • His poetry teaches love of humanity, patience, sacrifice and closeness to Allah. The musical instrument tanburo (danburo) is strongly linked with the singing of his kalam at his shrine.

Common Test Points

  • Urdu is the national language of Pakistan; Allama Iqbal is the national poet and Mirza Ghalib is famed for Urdu ghazal and letters.
  • The first Diwan (collection) of Urdu poetry in Deccan is linked with Wali Dakkani, called the father of Urdu poetry (Baba-e-Urdu is, however, Maulvi Abdul Haq — do not mix the two).
  • Anjuman Taraqqi-e-Urdu was founded for the promotion of Urdu; Maulvi Abdul Haq led it for many years.
  • In Sindh tests, questions often come from: Sindhi script and letters, Shah Jo Risalo and its heroines, Sachal Sarmast (known as "Shair-e-Haft Zuban", poet of seven languages), and the Indus Valley civilisation.
  • For Urdu, revise: ism/fail/harf, wahid-jama (singular-plural), muzakkar-muannas, muhavare and zarbul masal (proverbs).
