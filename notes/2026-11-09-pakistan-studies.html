The Pakistan Movement — From 1857 to 1947

Pakistan Studies Pakistan Movement 10 Practice MCQs

The Pakistan Movement is the most important topic of Pakistan Studies in SPSC, teaching and screening tests. It is the story of how the Muslims of the Subcontinent, who were a separate nation with their own religion, culture and laws, won a separate homeland. Learn the timeline below — almost every date in it has appeared in a test.

The Story in Short — Pehle Ye Samjhein

After the War of Independence (1857), the British blamed the Muslims and took away their power, jobs and land. Sir Syed Ahmed Khan told the Muslims to get modern education first and to stay away from the Congress, because the Congress spoke for the Hindu majority. This idea — that Muslims and Hindus are two separate nations — is called the Two-Nation Theory. Roman Urdu: Do Qaumi Nazariya ka matlab hai ke Musalman aur Hindu do alag qaumein hain — un ka mazhab, tehzeeb aur qanoon alag hai, is liye un ka mulk bhi alag hona chahiye.

From the Two-Nation Theory came the Muslim League (1906), the Lahore Resolution (1940) and finally, on 14 August 1947, the free country of Pakistan.

Timeline — 1857 Se 1947 Tak

1857 — War of Independence: the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was removed and the British took direct control of India.

1867 — Urdu–Hindi Controversy: in Benaras, Hindus demanded Hindi in place of Urdu. Sir Syed realised the two nations could not live under one system.

1875 — MAO College, Aligarh: Sir Syed founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College (later Aligarh Muslim University) for modern Muslim education.

1906 — Simla Deputation: a Muslim delegation led by Sir Aga Khan met Viceroy Lord Minto and asked for separate electorates.

1906 — Muslim League founded: the All-India Muslim League was founded in December 1906 at Dhaka. Its first president was Sir Aga Khan.

1916 — Lucknow Pact: the Congress and the Muslim League agreed to work together; the Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims.

1919 — Khilafat Movement: Indian Muslims protested against the British treatment of the Turkish Caliphate; it joined the Non-Cooperation Movement.

1928 — Nehru Report: the Congress proposed a future constitution with joint electorates. The Muslims rejected it.

1929 — Jinnah's Fourteen Points: Quaid-e-Azam's reply to the Nehru Report — separate electorates, Muslim representation and provincial autonomy.

1930 — Allahabad Address: Allama Iqbal proposed a separate Muslim state in the north-west of India.

1930–32 — Round Table Conferences: three conferences in London to discuss India's future constitution; Jinnah attended the first two.

1933 — The name "Pakistan": Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coined the name in his pamphlet "Now or Never".

1940 — Lahore Resolution: passed on 23 March 1940 in Lahore; presented by A. K. Fazlul Huq; it demanded separate states for the Muslims. It is also called the Pakistan Resolution.

1942 — Cripps Mission: the British offered dominion status after the war; the League and the Congress both rejected it.

1946 — Cabinet Mission: the last British plan for a united India; the League accepted it at first, the Congress did not.

1947 — 3 June Plan: Lord Mountbatten's plan to divide India into two states — India and Pakistan.

14 August 1947 — Independence: Pakistan came into being. Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah became the first Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan the first Prime Minister.

Key Leaders — Kaun Kya Tha?

Sir Syed Ahmed Khan — the thinker of the Two-Nation Theory; founded MAO College, Aligarh (1875).

Allama Iqbal — the poet-philosopher; gave the idea of a separate Muslim state (Allahabad Address, 1930).

Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah — the leader who led the movement to victory; presented the Fourteen Points (1929) and led the League from 1934.

Liaquat Ali Khan — Jinnah's right hand; became the first Prime Minister of Pakistan.

A. K. Fazlul Huq — presented the Lahore Resolution (1940).

Chaudhry Rahmat Ali — coined the name "Pakistan" (1933).

Sir Aga Khan — led the Simla Deputation (1906) and was the first president of the Muslim League.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Urdu–Hindi Controversy: 1867, Benaras.
  • MAO College, Aligarh: founded 1875 by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan.
  • Simla Deputation: 1906, led by Sir Aga Khan; asked for separate electorates.
  • Muslim League founded: December 1906, Dhaka; first president: Sir Aga Khan.
  • Lucknow Pact: 1916 — Congress accepted separate electorates.
  • Khilafat Movement: 1919; Nehru Report: 1928; Fourteen Points: 1929.
  • Allahabad Address: 1930, Allama Iqbal; Round Table Conferences: 1930–32.
  • Name "Pakistan" coined: 1933, Chaudhry Rahmat Ali, pamphlet "Now or Never".
  • Lahore Resolution: 23 March 1940, presented by A. K. Fazlul Huq.
  • Cripps Mission: 1942; Cabinet Mission: 1946; 3 June Plan: 1947.
  • Pakistan came into being: 14 August 1947.
