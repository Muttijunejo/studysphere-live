Newton's Laws of Motion — Study Notes

Physics Newton's Laws 10 Practice MCQs

Newton's three laws explain how forces change the motion of objects. They are among the most repeated Physics topics in SPSC and screening tests — learn the laws, the formulas and the common traps below.

Back to home page

Key Words — Pehle Ye Samajh Lein

Force — a push or pull that can change the motion of a body. SI unit: newton (N).

Mass (m) — the amount of matter in a body. SI unit: kilogram (kg). Mass never changes, wherever the body is.

Inertia — the resistance of a body to a change in its state of rest or motion. Roman Urdu: Jisam apni halat (sukoon ya harkat) badalne se rokta hai — yehi inertia hai.

Momentum (p) — the quantity of motion of a body.

p = m × v   ·   unit: kg·m/s

Weight (W) — the force of gravity on a body. Wazan mass se farq hai: wazan jagah ke saath badal sakta hai, mass nahi.

W = m × g   ·   g ≈ 9.8 m/s² on Earth

First Law — Law of Inertia

"A body stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed, unless a net force acts on it."

Roman Urdu: Agar koi bahar ki taaqat na lagay to ruka hua jisam ruka rahega aur chalta hua jisam barabar raftaar se chalta rahega.

Examples (test favourite):

  • A passenger falls forward when a moving bus stops suddenly — the body wants to keep moving.
  • A passenger falls backward when a bus starts suddenly — the body wants to stay at rest.
  • A tablecloth can be pulled quickly from under dishes — the dishes stay because of inertia of rest.
  • Seat belts protect you: they stop your body with the car instead of letting it keep moving.

Test point: Inertia depends only on mass — a heavier body has more inertia. It does not depend on speed or shape.

Second Law — Force = Mass × Acceleration

"The acceleration of a body is directly proportional to the force applied, and inversely proportional to its mass."

F = m × a   ·   1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

Roman Urdu: Jitni bari taaqat, utni ziada acceleration; jitna bara mass, utni kam acceleration.

Worked example: A force of 10 N acts on a mass of 2 kg.
a = F ÷ m = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m/s².

The second law is also the law of momentum change: F = change in momentum ÷ time.

Equations of motion (for constant acceleration):

v = u + at   ·   s = ut + ½at²   ·   v² = u² + 2as

Here u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = distance.

Third Law — Action and Reaction

"Every action has an equal and opposite reaction."

Roman Urdu: Har amal ka barabar aur ulta rad-e-amal hota hai.

Examples:

  • Rocket: hot gases push down (action), the rocket is pushed up (reaction).
  • Walking: your foot pushes the ground backward, the ground pushes you forward.
  • Gun recoil: the bullet goes forward, the gun kicks backward.
  • Swimming: you push water backward, the water pushes you forward.

Test trap: Action and reaction act on different bodies — that is why they never cancel each other out.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • g (acceleration due to gravity) ≈ 9.8 m/s² on Earth; zero in space.
  • Mass is the same on the Moon; weight on the Moon is about 1/6 of Earth's weight.
  • Velocity and force are vector quantities (size + direction); speed, mass and distance are scalar.
  • Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv² — energy of motion. Work: W = F × s, unit joule (J).
  • If no net force acts, a body keeps its velocity — this is the first law again.

Practice MCQs — 10 Questions

Try these first, then check the answer key at the end.

  1. Newton's first law is also called the law of:
    (a) Action   (b) Inertia   (c) Gravitation   (d) Momentum
  2. F = ma is the mathematical form of Newton's:
    (a) First law   (b) Second law   (c) Third law   (d) Law of gravitation
  3. A force of 10 N acts on a mass of 5 kg. The acceleration is:
    (a) 0.5 m/s²   (b) 2 m/s²   (c) 5 m/s²   (d) 50 m/s²
  4. The momentum of a 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s is:
    (a) 1.5 kg·m/s   (b) 5 kg·m/s   (c) 6 kg·m/s   (d) 9 kg·m/s
  5. Action and reaction forces act on:
    (a) The same body   (b) Different bodies   (c) The Earth only   (d) No body
  6. Inertia of a body depends on its:
    (a) Speed   (b) Mass   (c) Shape   (d) Colour
  7. A passenger falls forward when a moving bus stops suddenly. This is due to:
    (a) Friction   (b) Inertia of motion   (c) Gravity   (d) Air pressure
  8. A rocket moves forward because of:
    (a) Gravity   (b) Air pressure   (c) Reaction of the exhaust gases   (d) Friction
  9. The weight of a 10 kg mass on Earth (g = 10 m/s²) is:
    (a) 1 N   (b) 10 N   (c) 100 N   (d) 1000 N
  10. If no net force acts on a body, it will:
    (a) Stop at once   (b) Speed up   (c) Keep moving with the same velocity   (d) Fall down

Answer Key

1 — (b) Inertia  ·  2 — (b) Second law  ·  3 — (b) 2 m/s²  ·  4 — (c) 6 kg·m/s  ·  5 — (b) Different bodies  ·  6 — (b) Mass  ·  7 — (b) Inertia of motion  ·  8 — (c) Reaction of the exhaust gases  ·  9 — (c) 100 N  ·  10 — (c) Keep moving with the same velocity
