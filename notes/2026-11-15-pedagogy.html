Pedagogy — Teaching Methods

Pedagogy Teaching Methods 10 Practice MCQs

How teachers teach — the Lecture, Discussion, Demonstration, Project, Activity, Heuristic and Socratic methods: what each one is, when to use it, who introduced it, and which one is teacher-centred and which one is child-centred — in short simple notes with Roman Urdu explanations, then 10 practice MCQs with an answer key.

What Is a Teaching Method?

  • A teaching method is the way a teacher presents a lesson to students — how the teacher and the students work together in the classroom. Roman Urdu: tareeqa-e-tadrees — ustad lesson kis tareeqay se parhata hai.
  • Methods are of two main kinds: teacher-centred (the teacher talks, the students listen — for example, the Lecture Method) and child-centred (the student is active — for example, the Project and Activity Methods).
  • There is no single best method. A good teacher chooses the method that fits the topic, the class size and the students' age.

1. Lecture Method

  • The teacher talks and explains the lesson while the students listen and take notes. It is the oldest and most common method.
  • Best for: large classes and for giving a lot of information in a short time. Roman Urdu: bari class ko kam waqt mein zyada maloomat dene ke liye behtareen hai.
  • It is teacher-centred. Its main weakness: students remain passive listeners — they do not practise or discover anything themselves.

2. Discussion Method

  • The teacher gives a topic and the students discuss it together, giving their own ideas. The teacher only guides the discussion.
  • It develops thinking, speaking and listening skills and teaches students to respect other people's opinions.
  • Best for: small groups and topics that have more than one answer. It is slow and hard to control in a very large class.

3. Demonstration Method

  • The teacher shows and does something — for example, a science experiment — while the students watch carefully and then try it themselves. Roman Urdu: ustad kar ke dikhata hai, talib-e-ilm dekh kar seekhta hai.
  • Best for: teaching practical skills — science experiments, drawing, using tools, physical training.
  • Its strength: students see the real thing instead of only hearing about it, so they remember it better.

4. Project Method — William Kilpatrick (1918)

  • Students learn by completing a real, purposeful task — a project — such as making a model, growing a garden or writing a class magazine. It was introduced by William Kilpatrick in 1918, following the ideas of John Dewey.
  • Steps of a project: (1) creating a situation, (2) choosing the project, (3) planning, (4) executing (doing the work), (5) evaluating (judging the result).
  • It is child-centred and based on learning by doing. It builds planning, cooperation and problem-solving — but it takes a lot of time.

5. Activity Method — Learning by Doing

  • Students learn by doing activities themselves — handling objects, making things, acting, playing games — instead of only listening. Roman Urdu: bacha khud kar ke seekhta hai.
  • It is child-centred and works best with young children, who learn fastest through play and activity.

6. Heuristic Method — H. E. Armstrong

  • The student is placed in the position of a discoverer — he finds out the facts himself by observing and experimenting, instead of being told. It was introduced by H. E. Armstrong.
  • The word comes from Greek "heuriskein" — to discover. The teacher only gives the problem and guides; the student discovers the answer.
  • Best for science and mathematics. Its weakness: it is slow and cannot cover a long syllabus quickly.

7. Socratic Method — Questioning

  • Named after the Greek philosopher Socrates. The teacher asks a series of questions that lead the student, step by step, to the answer — the knowledge is drawn out of the student, not poured in.
  • Best for developing reasoning and clear thinking. It needs a skilled teacher and works best with small groups.

Teacher-Centred vs Child-Centred — Quick Table

  • Teacher-centred: Lecture Method — the teacher is active, the students listen.
  • Child-centred: Project Method, Activity Method, Heuristic Method — the students are active, the teacher guides.
  • In between: Discussion Method and Demonstration Method — both teacher and students are active.
  • Remember: lecture = teacher-centred is the most-asked pair in tests; project / activity = child-centred is the second most-asked.

Which Method, When?

  • Large class, little time, many facts → Lecture Method.
  • Teaching a science experiment or a skill → Demonstration Method.
  • Developing thinking and speaking → Discussion Method.
  • A real task, learning by doing, teamwork → Project Method.
  • Young children, learning through play → Activity Method.
  • Students discovering a rule or fact themselves → Heuristic Method.
  • Developing reasoning through questions → Socratic Method.

Common Test Traps

  • Project Method = Kilpatrick (1918); Heuristic Method = Armstrong; Socratic Method = Socrates. Do not mix the three names.
  • Lecture Method is teacher-centred; Project and Activity Methods are child-centred.
  • "Learning by doing" belongs to the Activity and Project Methods — and John Dewey's philosophy behind them.
  • Demonstration = the teacher shows, the students observe. Discussion = the students talk, the teacher guides.
  • Heuristic is slow but deep; Lecture is fast but passive — tests often ask for one weakness of each.

Practice MCQs — Teaching Methods (Q1–Q10)

  1. The Lecture Method is most suitable for:
    (a) Small groups of 5 students   (b) Large classes and quick delivery of facts   (c) Teaching a practical skill   (d) Slow learners only
  2. The Discussion Method mainly develops:
    (a) Memorising facts   (b) Writing speed   (c) Thinking and speaking skills   (d) Drawing skill
  3. In the Demonstration Method:
    (a) The teacher performs and students observe   (b) Students perform and the teacher watches   (c) Students read the textbook aloud   (d) The teacher gives a written test
  4. What is the first step of the Project Method?
    (a) Writing the examination   (b) Giving homework   (c) Marking the notebooks   (d) Creating a situation and choosing the project
  5. The Activity Method is based on:
    (a) Learning by doing   (b) Reading the textbook   (c) Listening to a lecture   (d) Copying notes from the board
  6. The Socratic Method mainly uses:
    (a) Long lectures   (b) Silent reading   (c) Questions that draw out knowledge   (d) Written tests
  7. Which of the following methods is teacher-centred?
    (a) Project Method   (b) Discussion Method   (c) Activity Method   (d) Lecture Method
  8. Which method is best for teaching a science experiment?
    (a) Lecture Method   (b) Demonstration Method   (c) Discussion Method   (d) Socratic Method
  9. The Heuristic Method was introduced by:
    (a) John Dewey   (b) William Kilpatrick   (c) Maria Montessori   (d) H. E. Armstrong
  10. What is the main disadvantage of the Lecture Method?
    (a) Students remain passive listeners   (b) It needs many charts   (c) It is slow for large classes   (d) It needs a laboratory
