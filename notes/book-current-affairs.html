Current Affairs & Who's Who Book — How to Prepare + Practice MCQs

Current Affairs · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

Every SPSC, FPSC and screening test has a current affairs part, and many students lose easy marks here because they do not know how to prepare. This book explains how current affairs are tested, how to prepare a little every day, and which stable facts — capitals, headquarters, founding years — you can learn once and use forever. The practice MCQs at the end are only on such stable facts.

How Current Affairs Are Tested

  • Most questions come from the last 6 to 12 months before the test: important appointments, awards, sports events, summits, agreements and records.
  • A second group is "Who's Who" — the names of people holding key offices in Pakistan and the world at the time of the test.
  • A third group is stable knowledge that never changes: world capitals, currencies, headquarters of organisations, founding years and famous firsts. These are the safest marks — learn them well.
  • Examiners repeat questions from past papers, so solving past papers is one of the best preparations.
  • Do not trust random social-media lists for "who is who" — names change. Always check an official source (see the next section).

How to Prepare Daily — A Simple Routine

  1. Read one good newspaper daily (for example Dawn) — only the front page, business page headlines and sports page are enough at the start. Give it 20–30 minutes.
  2. Keep a small notebook (or a notes app). Write one line per fact: date, event, names. Short notes are easy to revise before the test.
  3. Make weekly revision a habit — revise the week's notes every Sunday. Facts read once are forgotten; facts revised stay.
  4. Watch for these topics: federal and provincial budgets, Pakistan's agreements with other countries, major sports results, national awards (Nishan-e-Pakistan, Pride of Performance), new appointments to top offices, and international summits Pakistan attends.
  5. Before the test, revise the last 3 months more deeply and solve past papers of the same commission.

Pakistan's Key Offices — and How to Verify the Holders

Tests ask who holds the top offices of Pakistan on the test date. These names change after elections, appointments and retirements, so this book does not print names that may go out of date. Instead, learn the offices and verify the current holder from official sources close to your test:

  • Head of State: the President of Pakistan · Head of Government: the Prime Minister · then the Chief Justice of Pakistan, the Chairman Senate, the Speaker National Assembly, and the chiefs of the armed forces.
  • In the provinces: the Governor and the Chief Minister of each province, and the Chief Justice of each High Court.
  • Key institutions: the Governor of the State Bank of Pakistan, the Chairman of the Election Commission of Pakistan (Chief Election Commissioner), and the heads of bodies like NADRA, FBR and PCB come in tests again and again.
  • Where to verify: the official websites of the President, Prime Minister and National Assembly of Pakistan, the Supreme Court website, and the news pages of the Press Information Department (PID) and Associated Press of Pakistan (APP). For exams, also trust the latest editions of well-known printed "Who's Who" books over random websites.

World Organisations and Headquarters

  • United Nations (UN) — New York, USA · founded 1945
  • UNESCO — Paris, France · UNICEF — New York, USA · WHO — Geneva, Switzerland
  • World Bank and IMF — Washington, D.C., USA
  • International Court of Justice — The Hague, Netherlands
  • WTO (World Trade Organization) — Geneva, Switzerland · ILO — Geneva · FAO — Rome, Italy
  • OIC — Jeddah, Saudi Arabia · founded 1969 · SAARC — Kathmandu, Nepal · founded 1985
  • NATO — Brussels, Belgium · founded 1949 · European Union main institutions — Brussels, Belgium
  • ICRC (Red Cross) — Geneva, Switzerland · Interpol — Lyon, France

Major Events — The Types of Things Asked

For recent events, examiners do not ask small daily news. They ask about big, memorable events of the last few years. Learn to note these types whenever they happen:

  • Sports mega-events: Olympic Games, FIFA World Cup, Cricket World Cups — host country, winner, and any Pakistani achievement.
  • Global summits: UN General Assembly sessions, climate conferences (COP), G7/G20 and SCO meetings — host city and main decisions.
  • Pakistan milestones: national elections, budgets, major development projects, record-breaking achievements by Pakistanis, and international awards won by Pakistanis.
  • World records and firsts: first persons, longest/shortest, largest projects, space missions — these are favourite MCQ material.
  • Important days: for example, Pakistan Day (23 March), Independence Day (14 August), Defence Day (6 September), United Nations Day (24 October).

Keep your own dated notes for such events; one page per month is enough, and it becomes your personal current affairs book.
