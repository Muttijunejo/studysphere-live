The Indus River System — Pakistan's Six Rivers

Pakistan's rivers belong to the Indus river system. The Indus is the main river, and five rivers of Punjab join it: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej. Roman Urdu: Pakistan ke darya Indus nizaam ka hissa hain — Indus bara darya hai aur Punjab ke paanch darya us mein milte hain.

Key facts: The Indus is about 3,180 km long and is the longest river of Pakistan. The five Punjab rivers join together to form the Panjnad, which meets the Indus at Mithankot. The Kabul River joins the Indus at Attock.

Test trick: remember the five Punjab rivers as J-C-R-B-S — Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej. The Indus is not one of the five — it is the river they all join.

The Indus Waters Treaty 1960

The Indus Waters Treaty was signed on 19 September 1960 in Karachi by President Ayub Khan and Indian Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru. It was arranged by the World Bank. Roman Urdu: Ye muahida 1960 mein Karachi mein hua — Ayub Khan aur Nehru ne dastakhat kiye, World Bank ne madad ki.

Eastern rivers — given to India: Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.

Western rivers — given to Pakistan: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab.

Test trap: students mix up the two groups. Remember it as "R-B-S India, I-J-C Pakistan" — Ravi, Beas, Sutlej for India; Indus, Jhelum, Chenab for Pakistan.

Famous Dams of Pakistan

A dam is a wall built across a river to store water and make electricity. Roman Urdu: Band darya par deewar hota hai — paani jama karne aur bijli banane ke liye.

Test trick: the two biggest dams are the ones tests ask most — Tarbela on the Indus and Mangla on the Jhelum. If a question asks for a dam on the Jhelum, the answer is almost always Mangla.

Famous Barrages of Pakistan

A barrage is a low wall across a river that raises the water level so it can flow into canals for farming. Roman Urdu: Barrage darya par neechay band hota hai — paani ko nehron mein bhejne ke liye.

  • Sukkur Barrage — on the Indus, completed 1932; first called the Lloyd Barrage.
  • Kotri Barrage — on the Indus near Hyderabad, completed 1955.
  • Guddu Barrage — on the Indus near Kashmore, completed 1962.
  • Taunsa Barrage — on the Indus, completed 1958.
  • Chashma Barrage — on the Indus, completed 1971.
  • Jinnah Barrage — on the Indus at Kalabagh, completed 1946.

Test trick: almost every famous barrage of Pakistan stands on the Indus. The oldest of the list — and the one asked most — is Sukkur Barrage, 1932.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • The longest river of Pakistan is the Indus (about 3,180 km).
  • The five rivers of Punjab are Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; they form the Panjnad and meet the Indus at Mithankot.
  • The Kabul River joins the Indus at Attock.
  • The Indus Waters Treaty was signed on 19 September 1960 — eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India, western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan.
  • Tarbela Dam (Indus, 1976) is the largest earth-filled dam in the world; Mangla Dam (Jhelum, 1967) is the second famous dam.
  • Diamer-Bhasha Dam is under construction on the Indus.
  • Sukkur Barrage (1932, first called Lloyd Barrage) is the oldest famous barrage; Kotri (1955), Guddu (1962), Taunsa (1958), Chashma (1971), Jinnah (1946) all stand on the Indus.
  • The largest natural freshwater lake of Pakistan is Manchar Lake (Dadu, Sindh); Keenjhar Lake (Thatta) is the second largest and supplies water to Karachi.
  • Saiful Muluk is in Kaghan Valley (3,224 m high); Attabad Lake formed in 2010 after a landslide in Hunza.
  • The Indus rises in Tibet (Mount Kailash / Lake Mansarovar) and falls into the Arabian Sea near Karachi.
