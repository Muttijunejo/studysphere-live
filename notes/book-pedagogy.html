Pedagogy Book — Teaching Methods Notes & MCQs

Pedagogy · SPSC / FPSC / Teaching & Screening Tests · By MR Academy

Pedagogy is the science and art of teaching, and it is a major part of PST, JEST, EST and other teaching tests. This book explains the most tested topics in very simple words — Bloom's Taxonomy, classroom management, assessment, child psychology and lesson planning — with 15 practice MCQs at the end.

What is Pedagogy?

Pedagogy means the methods and principles a teacher uses to help students learn. Teaching is not only knowing a subject; a good teacher also knows how to explain it, how to keep students interested, and how to check whether learning has really happened.

  • A teacher-centred classroom means the teacher speaks most of the time and students only listen.
  • A learner-centred classroom means students take part actively — they ask questions, discuss, solve problems and do activities. Modern pedagogy supports this style.
  • Good teaching needs three things: subject knowledge, teaching method and understanding of the child.
  • The main goal of education is the all-round development of the child — mental, physical, social and moral.

Bloom's Taxonomy — The 6 Levels of Learning

Bloom's Taxonomy arranges learning from simple to difficult. It was given by Benjamin Bloom. The six levels (revised form) are:

  1. Remember — recall facts. Example: "Name the planets."
  2. Understand — explain in your own words. Example: "Why does the Moon shine?"
  3. Apply — use knowledge in a new situation. Example: solve a new maths sum with a learned formula.
  4. Analyze — break a thing into parts and compare. Example: compare two stories.
  5. Evaluate — give judgement with reasons. Example: "Which solution is better, and why?"
  6. Create — make something new. Example: write your own story or design an experiment.

The first three levels are called lower-order thinking and the last three higher-order thinking. Test questions often ask which level a classroom question belongs to, so learn the example verbs: define/list = Remember, explain = Understand, solve/use = Apply, compare = Analyze, judge = Evaluate, design/write = Create.

Classroom Management Tips

  • Make a few clear class rules with the students and follow them daily.
  • Start the lesson on time and keep every student busy with meaningful work — an idle class becomes noisy.
  • Use the student's name, move around the class, and make eye contact; this stops most problems before they start.
  • Praise good behaviour openly; correct bad behaviour calmly and privately. Positive reinforcement works better than punishment.
  • Change activities after short intervals — young children cannot sit and listen for very long.
  • Be fair to all students. Favouritism destroys discipline and trust.

Assessment — Formative and Summative

Assessment means checking what students have learned. There are two main types:

  • Formative assessment — done during the learning process. Examples: class questions, quizzes, homework, oral tests. Its purpose is to find weak points early and improve teaching. It is "assessment for learning".
  • Summative assessment — done at the end of a term or course. Examples: final examinations, board tests. It measures total achievement and is used for results and promotion. It is "assessment of learning".
  • A good test should be valid (it tests what it claims to test) and reliable (it gives similar results again and again).

Child Psychology Basics — Piaget's Stages

Jean Piaget studied how children's thinking develops. His four stages are asked in almost every teaching test:

  • Sensorimotor stage (0–2 years) — the baby learns through senses and movement; learns that things exist even when hidden (object permanence).
  • Preoperational stage (2–7 years) — language develops; thinking is self-centred (egocentric); the child cannot yet understand that quantity stays the same when shape changes.
  • Concrete operational stage (7–11 years) — the child can think logically about real, concrete things and understands conservation of number, mass and volume.
  • Formal operational stage (11 years and above) — abstract and scientific thinking begins; the young person can think about ideas, not only real objects.

The lesson for teachers: teach according to the child's stage. Small children learn best with real objects, pictures and activities — not with abstract lectures.

Lesson Planning Steps

A lesson plan is the teacher's written plan for one period. A simple lesson plan has these steps:

  1. Objectives — what students will learn by the end of the lesson (clear and measurable).
  2. Previous knowledge — start by asking what students already know about the topic.
  3. Introduction / motivation — a short question, story or activity to catch interest.
  4. Presentation — teach the new material step by step with examples and teaching aids.
  5. Practice / activity — students use the new knowledge themselves.
  6. Evaluation — a few questions to check whether the objectives were achieved.
  7. Homework / assignment — short related work for home.

Teaching aids (charts, models, the blackboard, real objects) make a lesson clear and interesting, and questions at the end of the lesson are a form of formative assessment.
