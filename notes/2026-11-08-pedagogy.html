Bloom's Taxonomy — Levels of Learning

Pedagogy Bloom's Taxonomy 10 Practice MCQs

Bloom's Taxonomy is one of the most repeated Pedagogy topics in SPSC, teaching and screening tests. It arranges learning aims from simple remembering to high-level creating. Learn the six levels, the 2001 revision and the classroom examples below.

Back to home page

What Is Bloom's Taxonomy? — Pehle Ye Samjhein

Benjamin Bloom and his team published the taxonomy in 1956. It is a ladder of six levels of thinking, from the easiest to the hardest. Roman Urdu: Bloom's Taxonomy seekhne ke maqasid ki aik seedhi hai — neeche se upar, aasan se mushkil.

Revised in 2001 by Anderson and Krathwohl (Bloom's student). They changed the nouns into action verbs and moved Creating to the top. In tests, "revised" almost always means the 2001 version.

Test point: The taxonomy belongs to the cognitive domain — the domain of knowledge and thinking. (Bloom also described the affective domain — feelings and attitudes — and the psychomotor domain — physical skills.)

The Six Levels — Neeche Se Upar

Learn them in order. The first three are lower-order thinking skills (LOTS); the last three are higher-order thinking skills (HOTS).

1. Remembering — recalling facts. Key verbs: list, define, name, recall, identify. Example: "Name the provinces of Pakistan."

2. Understanding — explaining ideas in your own words. Key verbs: explain, describe, summarise, interpret. Example: "Explain photosynthesis in your own words."

3. Applying — using knowledge in a new situation. Key verbs: apply, solve, use, demonstrate. Example: "Use the formula to find the area of this field."

4. Analysing — breaking a thing into parts and seeing how they connect. Key verbs: analyse, compare, classify, distinguish. Example: "Compare the 1956 and 1973 Constitutions."

5. Evaluating — judging and giving a reasoned opinion. Key verbs: evaluate, judge, justify, criticise. Example: "Judge which solution to water shortage is best, and why."

6. Creating — putting parts together to make something new. Key verbs: create, design, compose, plan. Example: "Design a lesson plan for teaching fractions."

Remembering → Understanding → Applying → Analysing → Evaluating → Creating

Original (1956) vs Revised (2001) — Farq Yaad Rakhein

Original top level: Evaluation — judging was the highest level in 1956.

Revised top level: Creating — Anderson and Krathwohl placed creating above evaluating in 2001.

Nouns became verbs: Knowledge became Remembering, Comprehension became Understanding, Application became Applying, Analysis became Analysing, Synthesis became Creating, Evaluation became Evaluating.

Test trap: If a question asks for the highest level and gives both "Evaluation" and "Creating" as options, the answer is Creating — because tests follow the revised taxonomy.

Why Teachers Use It

  • To write lesson objectives: a good objective starts with a verb from the level the teacher wants — for example, "Students will explain…" (Understanding) or "Students will design…" (Creating).
  • To make balanced test papers: a paper should have easy recall questions and also higher-order questions that test real thinking.
  • To move students upward: a teacher first checks remembering, then builds towards applying, analysing and creating.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Bloom's Taxonomy was published in 1956 by Benjamin Bloom.
  • It was revised in 2001 by Lorin Anderson and David Krathwohl.
  • There are six levels in the cognitive domain.
  • Lower-order thinking skills (LOTS): Remembering, Understanding, Applying.
  • Higher-order thinking skills (HOTS): Analysing, Evaluating, Creating.
  • "Explain in your own words" = Understanding; "compare" = Analysing; "judge" = Evaluating; "design a new plan" = Creating.

    (a) 1946   (b) 1956   (c) 1966   (d) 2001
  2. Who revised Bloom's Taxonomy in 2001?
    (a) Piaget and Vygotsky   (b) Anderson and Krathwohl   (c) Skinner and Pavlov   (d) Dewey and Herbart
  3. "Explain photosynthesis in your own words" tests which level?
    (a) Remembering   (b) Understanding   (c) Applying   (d) Creating
  4. A student uses a formula to solve a new problem. This is:
    (a) Remembering   (b) Understanding   (c) Applying   (d) Evaluating
  5. "Compare the 1956 and 1973 Constitutions" is a question of which level?
    (a) Remembering   (b) Applying   (c) Analysing   (d) Creating
  6. Which of the following is a higher-order thinking skill (HOTS)?
    (a) Remembering   (b) Understanding   (c) Applying   (d) Evaluating

    (a) Creating   (b) Synthesis   (c) Evaluation   (d) Analysis
  8. "Design a new timetable for your school" tests which level?
    (a) Applying   (b) Analysing   (c) Evaluating   (d) Creating
  9. Bloom's Taxonomy belongs to which domain of learning?
    (a) Affective domain   (b) Psychomotor domain   (c) Cognitive domain   (d) Social domain
  10. Which verb belongs to the Remembering level?
    (a) Design   (b) Judge   (c) List   (d) Compare
