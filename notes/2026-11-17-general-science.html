The Human Circulatory System — Heart & Blood

Your heart beats about 100,000 times every day without you ever telling it to. These notes explain, in simple English, how the heart pumps blood around the body, what blood is made of, why blood groups matter, and what blood pressure means — the facts that come up again and again in SPSC and screening tests.

What Is the Circulatory System? — Pehle Ye Samjhein

The circulatory system is the transport system of the body. It carries oxygen and food to every cell and takes away waste like carbon dioxide. Roman Urdu: Khoon ka nizam jism ke har hisse tak oxygen aur khana pohanchata hai, aur fazool cheezein wapas le aata hai.

It has three parts: (1) the heart — the pump; (2) the blood vessels — the pipes (arteries, veins and capillaries); and (3) the blood — the liquid that flows through them. Blood travels about 96,000 kilometres of vessels in the body.

The Heart — The Pump

The heart is a muscular organ about the size of your fist. It sits slightly to the left side of the chest, between the two lungs, and is protected by the ribs.

The heart has four chambers:

  • Right atrium — receives blood low in oxygen from the body.
  • Right ventricle — pumps that blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen.
  • Left atrium — receives oxygen-rich blood back from the lungs.
  • Left ventricle — the strongest chamber; pumps oxygen-rich blood to the whole body.

Valves are small doors between the chambers. They open one way only, so blood cannot flow backwards. The "lub-dub" sound of the heartbeat is the sound of these valves closing.

A healthy adult heart beats about 72 times per minute at rest — about 100,000 times a day — and pumps about 5 litres of blood every minute.

Blood Vessels — Arteries, Veins and Capillaries

1. Arteries: thick-walled vessels that carry blood away from the heart. They carry oxygen-rich blood (except the pulmonary artery, which carries blood to the lungs). You can feel your pulse in an artery — the wrist and the neck are the usual places.

2. Veins: thinner-walled vessels that carry blood back to the heart. They carry blood low in oxygen (except the pulmonary vein, which brings blood back from the lungs). Veins have valves to stop the blood flowing backwards.

3. Capillaries: the tiniest vessels, thinner than a hair. Here the real work happens — oxygen and food pass from the blood into the cells, and carbon dioxide and waste pass from the cells into the blood.

The largest artery in the body is the aorta; the largest vein is the vena cava.

Blood — What Is Blood Made Of?

An adult has about 5 to 6 litres of blood. Blood is about 55% plasma and 45% blood cells.

  • Plasma — the pale-yellow liquid part of blood. It carries food, hormones, salts and waste, and about 90% of plasma is water.
  • Red blood cells (RBCs) — they carry oxygen from the lungs to the body. They contain a red protein called haemoglobin, which holds the oxygen and gives blood its red colour. RBCs have no nucleus and live for about 120 days. They are made in the bone marrow.
  • White blood cells (WBCs) — the soldiers of the body. They fight germs and disease. Their number rises when you have an infection.
  • Platelets — tiny cell pieces that help the blood to clot when you are cut, so that you do not bleed too much.

Blood Groups — ABO and Rh

Human blood is divided into four main groups — A, B, AB and O — and each group is either Rh positive (+) or Rh negative (−).

O negative (O−) is the universal donor — it can be given to a person of any blood group in an emergency. AB positive (AB+) is the universal recipient — a person with AB+ blood can receive blood from any group.

Test trap: a wrong blood group can be dangerous, because the receiver's blood may reject the donor's blood. That is why the blood group is always checked before a transfusion.

Blood Pressure — 120/80

Blood pressure is the force of the blood pushing against the walls of the arteries. It is measured with an instrument called a sphygmomanometer.

The normal blood pressure of a healthy adult is 120/80 mmHg: 120 is the systolic pressure (when the heart beats and pushes blood out) and 80 is the diastolic pressure (when the heart rests between beats).

High blood pressure is called hypertension. It usually has no clear symptoms, so it is often called the "silent killer".

Double Circulation — Two Loops

The blood makes two loops through the heart:

  • Pulmonary circulation: heart → lungs → heart. Blood picks up oxygen in the lungs and drops off carbon dioxide.
  • Systemic circulation: heart → whole body → heart. Blood delivers oxygen and food to the body and collects waste.

Because the blood passes through the heart twice in one full round, this is called double circulation.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • The human heart has 4 chambers and beats about 72 times per minute at rest.
  • The heart is about the size of a fist and lies on the left side of the chest.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back to the heart.
  • The largest artery is the aorta; the largest vein is the vena cava.
  • Blood is 55% plasma; an adult has about 5–6 litres of blood.
  • Haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen; RBCs live about 120 days.
  • White blood cells fight disease; platelets help blood to clot.
  • O− is the universal donor; AB+ is the universal recipient.
  • Normal blood pressure is 120/80 mmHg, measured by a sphygmomanometer.
  • Blood goes through the heart twice in one round — this is double circulation.
