The Human Digestive System — How Food Becomes Energy

General Science Human Body 10 Practice MCQs

Every test of General Science asks about the human body, and digestion is one of the most repeated topics. These notes explain, in simple English, how the food you eat is broken down, absorbed into the blood and turned into energy — with the organs, juices and enzymes that examiners love to ask about.

What Is Digestion? — Pehle Ye Samjhein

Digestion is the process of breaking down big, complex food into small, simple, soluble parts that the body can absorb into the blood and use for energy, growth and repair. Roman Urdu: Hazma ka matlab hai khane ko chhote-chhote hisso mein torna, taake jism use jazb kar ke energy bana sake.

Food is digested in two ways: mechanical digestion (teeth chew it, the stomach churns it) and chemical digestion (enzymes and acids break it down). The whole journey from mouth to the end takes about 24 to 72 hours.

The Journey of Food — Khana Kahan Se Guzarta Hai?

1. Mouth: Teeth cut and grind the food. Saliva from the salivary glands softens it, and the enzyme salivary amylase (ptyalin) starts digesting starch. The tongue rolls the food into a ball (bolus) and pushes it down. An adult has 32 teeth — incisors (cutting), canines (tearing), premolars and molars (grinding).

2. Oesophagus: a muscular tube about 25 cm long. Wave-like muscle movements called peristalsis push the food down to the stomach.

3. Stomach: a bag-like organ that churns the food and mixes it with gastric juice. The juice contains hydrochloric acid (HCl), which kills germs and helps the enzyme pepsin digest proteins. The partly digested food is now a thick liquid called chyme.

4. Small intestine: the longest part of the gut (about 6–7 metres). Here most digestion and absorption happen. Bile from the liver breaks big fat drops into small ones; pancreatic juice finishes digesting proteins, fats and starch; and the inner wall is covered with tiny finger-like parts called villi, which absorb the digested food into the blood.

5. Large intestine: about 1.5 metres long. It absorbs water and salts from what is left. Useful bacteria live here and make some vitamins.

6. Rectum and anus: the undigested waste (faeces) is stored in the rectum and leaves the body through the anus.

Helper Organs — Kaun Madad Karta Hai?

Liver — the largest internal organ of the body. It makes bile, which is stored in the gall bladder and helps digest fats. It also stores glucose and cleans the blood.

Gall bladder — a small bag under the liver that stores bile and releases it into the small intestine.

Pancreas — makes pancreatic juice for digestion AND the hormone insulin, which controls blood sugar. A lack of insulin causes diabetes.

Salivary glands — make saliva; the enzyme amylase in saliva starts starch digestion in the mouth.

Important Enzymes — Test Mein Bar Bar Aate Hain

  • Salivary amylase (ptyalin) — in saliva; digests starch into sugar, in the mouth.
  • Pepsin — in the stomach; digests proteins; it works in acid (HCl).
  • Trypsin — in pancreatic juice; digests proteins in the small intestine.
  • Lipase — digests fats into fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Bile — not an enzyme, but it breaks big fat drops into small drops so lipase can work.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Digestion begins in the mouth; most absorption happens in the small intestine.
  • An adult has 32 teeth; a child has 20 milk teeth.
  • The small intestine is about 6–7 metres long; the large intestine is about 1.5 metres.
  • The stomach acid is hydrochloric acid (HCl); it kills germs and activates pepsin.
  • Villi are the finger-like parts in the small intestine that absorb food into the blood.
  • The liver is the largest internal organ; it makes bile.
  • The pancreas makes insulin — a lack of insulin causes diabetes.
  • Peristalsis is the wave-like movement that pushes food through the gut.
  • The large intestine absorbs water and salts.
  • Food stays in the stomach for about 2–4 hours.

    (a) nitric acid   (b) sulphuric acid   (c) hydrochloric acid   (d) citric acid
  5. Bile, which helps digest fats, is made by the:
    (a) pancreas   (b) stomach   (c) gall bladder   (d) liver
  6. Most digestion and absorption of food take place in the:
    (a) stomach   (b) large intestine   (c) small intestine   (d) mouth

    (a) villi   (b) alveoli   (c) nephrons   (d) neurons

    (a) stomach   (b) small intestine   (c) large intestine   (d) liver

  10. The enzyme in the stomach that digests proteins is:
    (a) amylase   (b) lipase   (c) pepsin   (d) trypsin
