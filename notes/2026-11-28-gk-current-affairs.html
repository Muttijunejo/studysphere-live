Great Personalities of Pakistan — Leaders, Scientists and Heroes

General Knowledge Personalities 10 Practice MCQs

One or two questions about famous Pakistanis come in almost every GK paper of SPSC and screening tests — who they were, what they did and the dates of their birth, death and great achievements. These notes give you the nine personalities tests ask about most, in simple English with Roman Urdu help.

1. Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah — Founder of Pakistan

Muhammad Ali Jinnah is the founder of Pakistan — Quaid-e-Azam means "Great Leader". Roman Urdu: Quaid-e-Azam ka matlab hai "Azeem Leader" — woh Pakistan ke bani hain.

He was born on 25 December 1876 in Karachi and died on 11 September 1948 in Karachi, only about a year after independence. He was Pakistan's first Governor-General (14 August 1947 – 11 September 1948).

As a barrister trained in London, he led the Muslim League, and his 14 August 1947 address to the Constituent Assembly promised freedom of religion and equality for all citizens. His motto for the nation was "Unity, Faith and Discipline" — learn the three words in this order, because tests ask them as one line.

Born: 25 December 1876, Karachi  ·  Died: 11 September 1948, Karachi  ·  First Governor-General of Pakistan

2. Allama Muhammad Iqbal — The National Poet

Allama Muhammad Iqbal is Pakistan's national poet and the thinker of Pakistan — the Shair-e-Mashriq (Poet of the East). Roman Urdu: Iqbal ko "Shair-e-Mashriq" kaha jata hai — Mashriq (East) ka shair.

He was born on 9 November 1877 in Sialkot and died on 21 April 1938 in Lahore. His birthday, 9 November, is celebrated as Iqbal Day.

In his famous Allahabad Address of 1930, he first presented the idea of a separate homeland for the Muslims of India — the idea that became Pakistan in 1947. His famous books are Bang-e-Dra, Bal-e-Jibril and Zarb-e-Kaleem; his poems Shikwa and Jawab-e-Shikwa are the most asked-about in tests.

Born: 9 November 1877, Sialkot  ·  Died: 21 April 1938, Lahore  ·  Allahabad Address: 1930

3. Liaquat Ali Khan — The First Prime Minister

Liaquat Ali Khan was the first Prime Minister of Pakistan (15 August 1947 – 16 October 1951). He was Quaid-e-Azam's right-hand man and is known as Quaid-e-Millat.

He was born on 1 October 1895 in Karnal (in present-day India). He presented the Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly on 12 March 1949 — the document that later became the preamble of the Constitution.

He was assassinated on 16 October 1951 in Rawalpindi, at a public meeting in Company Bagh — the park was later renamed Liaquat Bagh after him. Roman Urdu: Liaquat Ali Khan ko 16 October 1951 ko Rawalpindi mein shaheed kar diya gaya tha.

First Prime Minister: 1947–1951  ·  Objectives Resolution: 12 March 1949  ·  Assassinated: 16 October 1951, Rawalpindi

4. Fatima Jinnah — Madar-e-Millat

Fatima Jinnah, the younger sister of Quaid-e-Azam, is known as Madar-e-Millat — "Mother of the Nation". Roman Urdu: Madar-e-Millat ka matlab hai "Qaum ki Maa".

She was born on 31 July 1893 in Karachi and died on 9 July 1967. She stood beside her brother through the whole Pakistan Movement and, after his death, became a voice for democracy.

In 1965 she contested the presidential election against General Ayub Khan — the first time a woman ran for the highest office in Pakistan. Tests often ask this: Fatima Jinnah vs Ayub Khan, 1965.

5. Zulfikar Ali Bhutto — Founder of the PPP

Zulfikar Ali Bhutto was born on 5 January 1928 in Larkana, Sindh. He founded the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) on 30 November 1967 in Lahore.

He was Pakistan's Prime Minister from 1973 to 1977. The Constitution of 1973 — the one still in force today — was passed under his government. He also started Pakistan's nuclear programme and gave the country its first democratic constitution.

He was removed from office on 5 July 1977 and executed on 4 April 1979. His daughter, Benazir Bhutto, later became the first woman Prime Minister of Pakistan (and of any Muslim country) in 1988.

6. Dr Abdul Qadeer Khan — Father of the Nuclear Programme

Dr Abdul Qadeer Khan (Dr A. Q. Khan) is known as the father of Pakistan's nuclear programme. Roman Urdu: Dr A. Q. Khan ko Pakistan ke atomic programme ka baani kaha jata hai.

He was born on 1 April 1936 in Bhopal and died on 10 October 2021 in Islamabad. He built the Kahuta Research Laboratories (KRL), where Pakistan's nuclear programme was developed.

Under his work, Pakistan carried out its first nuclear tests on 28 May 1998 at Chagai, Balochistan — the day is celebrated as Youm-e-Takbeer (Day of Greatness). Do not confuse him with Dr Abdus Salam, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979 — Salam was a physicist, A. Q. Khan built the nuclear programme.

Born: 1 April 1936, Bhopal  ·  Died: 10 October 2021  ·  Nuclear tests: 28 May 1998 (Youm-e-Takbeer)

7. Abdul Sattar Edhi — Angel of Mercy

Abdul Sattar Edhi was Pakistan's greatest social worker, known around the world as the "Angel of Mercy". Roman Urdu: Edhi Sahab ko "Angel of Mercy" — Rehmat ka Farishta — kaha jata hai.

He was born on 28 February 1928 in Bantva, Gujarat (in present-day India) and died on 8 July 2016 in Karachi. In 1951 he started the Edhi Foundation with almost nothing — one small dispensary and a single second-hand ambulance.

Today the Edhi Foundation runs the largest volunteer ambulance service in the world, with thousands of ambulances, plus orphanages, old-age homes, hospitals and shelters across Pakistan. He never took a salary and lived in a small room next to his office.

8. Arfa Karim — The Child Prodigy of Computers

Arfa Karim Randhawa was a child genius of computers. She was born on 2 February 1995 in Faisalabad.

In 2005, at the age of only 9 years, she became the youngest Microsoft Certified Professional (MCP) in the world. Microsoft's founder, Bill Gates, invited her to visit Microsoft's headquarters in the USA.

She died very young, on 14 January 2012, at the age of 16. The Arfa Software Technology Park in Lahore is named after her. Roman Urdu: Arfa Karim sirf 9 saal ki umar mein duniya ki sab se chhoti Microsoft Certified Professional bani thi.

9. Imran Khan — Captain, Philanthropist, Prime Minister

Imran Khan was born on 5 October 1952 in Lahore. As captain of Pakistan's cricket team, he led Pakistan to win the Cricket World Cup in 1992 — Pakistan's only World Cup win.

After cricket, he built the Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital in Lahore (opened 1994), named after his mother, where poor cancer patients are treated free of cost. He also founded Namal University in Mianwali.

He entered politics with the party Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), founded in 1996, and became Pakistan's Prime Minister from 2018 to 2022.

10. Test Traps — Ghaltiyan Jo Tests Mein Repeat Hoti Hain

  • Trap 1 — two Khans: Dr A. Q. Khan built the nuclear programme; Dr Abdus Salam won the Nobel Prize in Physics (1979). Tests put one name in the other's question on purpose.
  • Trap 2 — two Bhuttos: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto founded the PPP in 1967 and was Prime Minister 1973–77; his daughter Benazir Bhutto became Prime Minister in 1988. Do not swap father and daughter.
  • Trap 3 — Jinnah's dates: born 25 December 1876, died 11 September 1948. The two dates are asked separately — learn them as a pair.
  • Trap 4 — Iqbal's city: Iqbal was born in Sialkot, not Lahore. He died in Lahore — the two cities are swapped in the wrong options.
  • Trap 5 — Liaquat's year: Liaquat Ali Khan was assassinated in 1951, not 1948 (the year Jinnah died) and not 1958 (the year of the first martial law).
  • Trap 6 — Edhi's year: the Edhi Foundation started in 1951 — the same year Liaquat Ali Khan was assassinated. Two different facts, one year.
  • Trap 7 — Arfa's age: Arfa Karim was 9 when she became an MCP — not 11, not 12. The number 9 is the whole point of the question.
