Quadratic Equations — Solve by Factorisation & Formula

A quadratic equation appears in almost every screening test — sometimes directly, sometimes hidden inside a word problem. These notes explain, in simple English, what a quadratic equation is, how to solve it by factorisation and by the formula, what the discriminant tells you, and how the sum and product of the roots work.

What Is a Quadratic Equation? — Pehle Ye Samjhein

A quadratic equation is an equation of the form:

ax² + bx + c = 0   (where a ≠ 0)

The word "quadratic" comes from "quadratus", the Latin word for square — because the highest power of x is 2. Roman Urdu: Jis equation mein x ki sab se bari taqat 2 ho, use quadratic equation kehte hain.

In ax² + bx + c = 0: a is the coefficient of x², b is the coefficient of x, and c is the constant term. Example: in 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0, a = 2, b = −5 and c = 3.

The values of x that satisfy the equation are called its roots (or solutions). A quadratic equation has at most two roots.

Solving by Factorisation

Factorise the left side, then set each factor equal to zero. If a product is zero, at least one factor must be zero.

Example 1: x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Find two numbers that add to −5 and multiply to 6: −2 and −3. So (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, which gives x = 2 or x = 3.

Example 2: x² − 9 = 0. This is a difference of two squares: (x − 3)(x + 3) = 0, so x = 3 or x = −3.

Example 3: x² + 7x + 12 = 0. Two numbers that add to 7 and multiply to 12 are 3 and 4: (x + 3)(x + 4) = 0, so x = −3 or x = −4.

Test trick: when a = 1, look for two numbers that add to b and multiply to c. When a ≠ 1, first try to take out a common factor, or use the formula below.

The Quadratic Formula

When factorisation is difficult, use the quadratic formula — it works for every quadratic equation:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

Example: 2x² + 5x − 3 = 0. Here a = 2, b = 5, c = −3.

x = (−5 ± √(25 − 4 × 2 × (−3))) / (2 × 2) = (−5 ± √49) / 4 = (−5 ± 7) / 4.

So x = (−5 + 7) / 4 = 1/2 or x = (−5 − 7) / 4 = −3.

Check: put x = 1/2 back in: 2 × (1/4) + 5 × (1/2) − 3 = 1/2 + 5/2 − 3 = 0. ✓ Always check a root by putting it back into the equation — the left side must become zero.

The Discriminant — Nature of Roots

The part under the square root in the formula is called the discriminant:

D = b² − 4ac

It tells you, without solving, what kind of roots the equation has:

  • D > 0 — two different real roots.
  • D = 0 — two equal roots (one repeated root).
  • D < 0 — no real roots (the roots are not real numbers).

Example: x² − 4x + 4 = 0 → D = 16 − 16 = 0, so the roots are equal: x = 2 (twice). Example: x² + x + 1 = 0 → D = 1 − 4 = −3 < 0, so there are no real roots.

Sum and Product of Roots

If the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0 are α and β, then:

Sum of roots: α + β = −b/a   ·   Product of roots: αβ = c/a

Example: x² + 3x − 10 = 0 has roots 2 and −5. Sum = 2 + (−5) = −3 = −b/a ✓. Product = 2 × (−5) = −10 = c/a ✓.

Forming an equation from its roots: if the roots are known, the equation is x² − (sum of roots)x + (product of roots) = 0. Roots 4 and −1 give x² − 3x − 4 = 0.

Test trap: if the product of the roots is negative, the roots have opposite signs — one positive, one negative.

Completing the Square

A third method: make the left side a perfect square.

Example: x² + 6x + 5 = 0 → (x + 3)² − 9 + 5 = 0 → (x + 3)² = 4 → x + 3 = ±2, so x = −1 or x = −5.

This method is the one from which the quadratic formula itself is derived — so if you know completing the square, you can always re-derive the formula.

Quick Facts — Test Mein Aane Wale Points

  • Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0, a ≠ 0; the degree is 2 and there are at most 2 roots.
  • Discriminant D = b² − 4ac decides the nature of the roots: D > 0 two real roots, D = 0 equal roots, D < 0 no real roots.
  • Sum of roots = −b/a; product of roots = c/a.
  • If x = 2 is a root, then (x − 2) is a factor of the equation.
  • Roots are equal when D = 0 — for example x² − 4x + 4 = 0 has the repeated root 2.
  • Difference of two squares: x² − a² = (x − a)(x + a).
  • If the product of the roots is negative, the roots have opposite signs.
  • Factorisation trick (when a = 1): find two numbers that add to b and multiply to c.
  • A quadratic with roots 0 and 5 is x² − 5x = 0.
  • Always check a root by putting it back into the equation — the left side must become 0.

Practice MCQs — 10 Questions

Try these first, then check the answer key at the end.

  1. Which is the standard form of a quadratic equation?
    (a) ax + b = 0   (b) ax² + bx + c = 0   (c) ax³ + bx = 0   (d) x² + y² = 0
  2. What are the roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0?
    (a) 1 and 6   (b) 2 and 3   (c) −2 and −3   (d) 5 and 1
  3. What is the discriminant of x² − 4x + 4 = 0?
    (a) 0   (b) 4   (c) 16   (d) −16
  4. What is the sum of the roots of x² + 3x − 10 = 0?
    (a) 3   (b) −3   (c) 10   (d) −10
  5. What is the product of the roots of 2x² − 8x + 6 = 0?
    (a) 6   (b) 3   (c) −3   (d) 8
  6. If the discriminant of a quadratic equation is less than 0, the roots are:
    (a) two equal real roots   (b) two different real roots   (c) no real roots   (d) one real root
  7. What are the roots of x² − 9 = 0?
    (a) 3 only   (b) −3 only   (c) 3 and −3   (d) 9 and −9
  8. Factorise x² + 7x + 12.
    (a) (x + 2)(x + 6)   (b) (x + 3)(x + 4)   (c) (x + 1)(x + 12)   (d) (x − 3)(x − 4)
  9. By the quadratic formula, the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0 are:
    (a) (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a   (b) (−b ± √(b² + 4ac)) / a   (c) (b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a   (d) (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / a
  10. What are the roots of x² − 3x − 10 = 0?
    (a) 5 and 2   (b) −5 and 2   (c) 5 and −2   (d) −5 and −2
