Pedagogy MCQs

By MR Academy — Published 2026-11-08 — Last updated 2026-11-08

These are original Practice MCQs on Pedagogy (teaching methods, lesson planning, classroom management and assessment) for PST, JEST, SST, EST and other teacher-test preparation. They are practice material written by MR Academy, not official past-paper questions. Click an option to select it, then press Show Answers to see the correct answers, explanations and your score.

1. The word "pedagogy" is mainly concerned with:

Answer: A. Pedagogy means the art and science of teaching — the methods and practice of how a teacher teaches.

2. Which teaching method is most suitable for teaching a practical skill, such as using a microscope?

Answer: B. In the demonstration method the teacher shows the process step by step, so a practical skill is learned by watching and then doing.

3. A lesson plan is prepared:

Answer: C. A lesson plan is the teacher's advance outline of objectives, activities and assessment, written before the lesson is taught.

4. Which of these is a student-centred method of teaching?

Answer: D. In group discussion the students actively share and build ideas, so the learning centres on them instead of only on the teacher.

5. The first thing a teacher should decide when writing a lesson plan is:

Answer: A. Objectives state what learners will be able to do by the end of the lesson; activities and questions are then chosen to reach them.

6. Formative assessment is carried out:

Answer: C. Formative assessment happens during learning — through questions, quizzes and observation — so the teacher can adjust teaching in time.

7. Summative assessment is usually done:

Answer: B. Summative assessment, such as a final examination, judges the overall achievement of learners at the end of a period of learning.

8. Classroom management mainly aims at:

Answer: D. Good classroom management organises time, attention and behaviour so that real learning can take place smoothly.

9. Which question checks the LOWEST level of Bloom's taxonomy?

Answer: C. "Define" asks only for remembering a fact, which is the Knowledge/Remember level — the lowest level of Bloom's taxonomy.

10. In Bloom's original taxonomy, the highest level is:

Answer: A. Bloom's original (1956) order ends with Evaluation — judging ideas against criteria. The revised taxonomy later placed Creating at the top.

11. "Set induction" at the start of a lesson means:

Answer: D. Set induction is the opening move of a lesson: it focuses students' attention and connects the new topic with what they already know.

12. Which is an example of positive reinforcement?

Answer: B. Reinforcement strengthens a behaviour; praise given right after a correct answer is positive reinforcement.

13. A micro-teaching session focuses on:

Answer: A. In micro-teaching a trainee practises a single skill, such as questioning, with a small group for a few minutes and then receives feedback.

14. Objective-type questions are considered fair in marking because:

Answer: C. Objective items have one fixed correct answer, so scoring stays the same no matter who marks the paper.

15. The main purpose of asking questions in class is to:

Answer: D. Good questions reveal what students have understood and push them to think, not merely to repeat memorised words.

16. Which activity best develops speaking skills in a language class?

Answer: B. Role play makes learners actually speak in real-life situations, which builds fluency and confidence.

17. Homework should mainly be given so that students:

Answer: C. Homework is useful when it consolidates class learning through practice — not when it is used as a burden or a punishment.

18. The blueprint (table of specifications) of a test shows:

Answer: A. A test blueprint maps topics against question types and marks before the paper is set, keeping the paper balanced.

19. The most reliable way to assess a student's practical science skill is to:

Answer: B. Practical skill can only be judged directly, by watching the learner actually perform the task — this is performance assessment.

20. "Wait time" after asking a question means the teacher should:

Answer: D. A short pause after a question gives more students time to think, and the quality and number of answers improve.

21. Teaching a grammar rule by the deductive method means:

Answer: C. Deductive teaching goes from rule to examples; the inductive method is the reverse — examples first, and the rule is discovered.

22. A test is called valid when it:

Answer: A. Validity means the test actually measures the intended learning; a test can be long or short and still be invalid.

23. Reliability of a test means that it:

Answer: D. A reliable test produces nearly the same result for the same learner when repeated under similar conditions.

24. The lecture method is most useful when:

Answer: B. A lecture can deliver content quickly to many students, but it keeps learners passive — so it suits information delivery, not skill practice.

25. A teacher tests students' previous knowledge at the start of a lesson so that he can:

Answer: A. Teaching starts from the known; checking previous knowledge lets the teacher build the new topic on a firm base.

26. Which seating arrangement best supports group work?

Answer: D. Cluster seating lets group members see and talk to one another, which cooperative tasks require.

27. In assessment, a portfolio is:

Answer: B. A portfolio collects samples of a learner's work across a term, so growth and effort can be assessed — not just one test score.

28. The "chalk and talk" approach is criticised mainly because:

Answer: C. Teacher-dominated talk gives students little chance to practise, discuss or apply, so learning stays shallow.

29. Bloom's taxonomy of educational objectives belongs to the:

Answer: A. Bloom's 1956 taxonomy classifies cognitive (thinking) objectives; Krathwohl covered the affective domain and Simpson the psychomotor.

30. An essay-type test is best for measuring a student's:

Answer: B. Essays let learners organise, argue and express ideas — higher-order abilities that objective items cannot easily capture.

31. "Probing questions" are asked in order to:

Answer: D. Probing (follow-up) questions push a learner to explain, justify or extend an initial answer.

32. Classroom rules work best when they are:

Answer: C. A few positively stated rules, understood by everyone and applied fairly every time, manage behaviour far better than long lists.

33. Diagnostic assessment is used to:

Answer: B. Diagnostic assessment pinpoints exactly where and why a learner is struggling, so remedial teaching can be planned.

34. Which of the following is an audio-visual aid?

Answer: A. Audio-visual aids combine sound and picture, such as a video; a handout and a blackboard are visual aids, and speech alone is audio.

35. Developing the blackboard properly during a lesson means:

Answer: C. A good board summary grows with the lesson: key points written clearly and in sequence so students can follow and copy them.

36. Cooperative learning works best when:

Answer: D. Positive interdependence with individual accountability — everyone contributes and everyone learns — is the heart of cooperative learning.

37. A teacher asks: "What would happen if there were no rain for a year?" This question mainly develops:

Answer: B. An open hypothetical question has no single memorised answer; students must reason about consequences and imagine.

38. "Learning by doing" is the main principle behind the:

Answer: C. In activity and project methods students learn through direct experience — doing real tasks rather than only listening.

39. The final step of a good lesson is usually:

Answer: D. Ending with recapitulation — quick review questions — checks whether the objectives were achieved and fixes the main points in memory.

40. The term "curriculum" is broader than "syllabus" because the curriculum includes:

Answer: A. A syllabus lists the content of a subject; the curriculum covers all planned experiences — subjects, activities, sports and values.
