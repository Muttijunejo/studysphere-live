English Tenses MCQs

By MR Academy — Published 2026-11-08 — Last updated 2026-11-08

These are original Practice MCQs on English Tenses — present, past and future forms, the perfect and continuous tenses, time markers (since, for, already, yet) and common errors — for PST, JEST, SST, SPSC and other test preparation. They are practice material written by MR Academy, not official past-paper questions. Click an option to select it, then press Show Answers to see the correct answers, explanations and your score.

1. She ___ to school every day.

Answer: A. A daily routine takes the present simple; with 'she' the verb takes -s: goes.

2. Look! The children ___ in the garden.

Answer: B. 'Look!' shows the action is happening now, so present continuous (are playing) is used.

3. I ___ my work already.

Answer: C. 'Already' with a just-completed action takes the present perfect: have finished.

4. He ___ in Karachi since 2019.

Answer: D. An action that began in the past and still continues with 'since' takes present perfect continuous: has been living.

5. We ___ TV when the power went off.

Answer: C. A longer action going on when a short past action interrupted it takes the past continuous: were watching.

6. The train ___ before we reached the station.

Answer: A. Of two past actions, the one completed earlier takes the past perfect: had left.

7. By next June, she ___ her course.

Answer: D. 'By next June' points to an action finished before a future time — future perfect: will have completed.

8. It ___ heavily tomorrow, according to the weather forecast.

Answer: B. A future prediction takes will + first form of the verb: will rain.

9. He ___ his purse yesterday.

Answer: B. 'Yesterday' fixes the action in the past, so the past simple form 'lost' is used.

10. This time tomorrow, we ___ to Lahore.

Answer: D. An action in progress at a stated future time takes the future continuous: will be travelling.

11. Water ___ at 100 degrees Celsius.

Answer: A. A scientific fact or universal truth is always stated in the present simple: boils.

12. She has just ___ her lunch.

Answer: C. Present perfect (has) needs the third form (past participle) of the verb: eaten.

13. I ___ him for ten years.

Answer: D. 'Know' is a state verb; with a period up to now ('for ten years') the present perfect have known is correct.

14. The sun ___ in the east.

Answer: C. A universal truth takes the present simple; with 'the sun' the verb takes -s: rises.

15. Please be quiet! The baby ___ .

Answer: B. The action is happening at the moment of speaking, so present continuous: is sleeping.

16. They ___ the final match last Sunday.

Answer: A. 'Last Sunday' is a finished past time, so the past simple 'won' is used.

17. She ___ for the bus for two hours.

Answer: A. Waiting started earlier and is still going on ('for two hours') — present perfect continuous: has been waiting.

18. Mother ___ dinner before the guests arrived.

Answer: C. Cooking was completed before another past action (guests arrived), so past perfect: had cooked.

19. I think our team ___ the match.

Answer: B. A future hope or prediction after 'I think' takes will + verb: will win.

20. He usually ___ tea in the morning.

Answer: D. 'Usually' shows a habit, so present simple; with 'he' the verb takes -s: drinks.

21. ___ you ever ___ a camel ride?

Answer: B. Experience up to now with 'ever' takes present perfect: Have you ever taken...? (take–took–taken).

22. The baby ___ all night last night.

Answer: A. A finished action of last night takes the past simple: cried.

23. By 2035, they ___ the new bridge.

Answer: D. Completion before a future deadline ('by 2035') takes the future perfect: will have built.

24. She has not ___ her assignment yet.

Answer: C. 'Has not' needs the past participle, and 'yet' is the classic present-perfect signal: finished.

25. We ___ to Murree next Friday; the seats are booked.

Answer: C. A fixed future arrangement (seats already booked) is expressed with the present continuous: are going.

26. He ___ his leg while playing cricket yesterday.

Answer: D. A single completed past accident takes the past simple: broke (break–broke–broken).

27. It ___ since morning.

Answer: A. Rain began in the morning and is still falling ('since morning') — present perfect continuous: has been raining.

28. Ali ___ his lessons every evening.

Answer: B. A regular evening habit takes the present simple; with 'Ali' the verb takes -s: revises.

29. The teacher ___ the chapter before the bell rang.

Answer: D. Finishing happened before the other past event (bell rang), so past perfect: had finished.

30. They ___ a new school next year; the plan is ready.

Answer: B. A decided future plan is expressed with 'be going to': are going to build.

31. She ___ to the bazaar when I saw her.

Answer: C. An action in progress at a past moment (when I saw her) takes the past continuous: was going.

32. I ___ my keys, so I cannot open the door.

Answer: A. A past action with a present result (cannot open the door now) takes the present perfect: have lost.

33. He ___ in this bank for five years and still works here.

Answer: A. Work continuing up to now over 'for five years' takes present perfect continuous: has been working.

34. The train ___ at 9 o'clock every morning.

Answer: D. A fixed timetable is stated in the present simple even though each departure is regular: leaves.

35. At midnight tonight, we ___ fast asleep.

Answer: B. A state in progress at a future moment takes the future continuous: will be (sleeping).

36. She has never ___ to Islamabad.

Answer: C. After 'has' we need the participle of 'be': been. 'Has never been to' means she has never visited it.

37. The boys ___ their homework before their father came home.

Answer: B. The homework was completed before the past event (father came), so past perfect: had done.

38. Listen! The phone ___ .

Answer: C. 'Listen!' means the sound is happening right now — present continuous: is ringing.

39. Which sentence is grammatically correct?

Answer: D. With he/she/it we use 'doesn't' + first form of the verb: He doesn't like coffee.

40. Which sentence is grammatically correct?

Answer: A. An illness continuing from a past point ('since Monday') up to now takes the present perfect: has been ill.
