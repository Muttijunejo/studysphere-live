Bloom's Taxonomy MCQs

By MR Academy — Published 2026-11-08 — Last updated 2026-11-08

These are original Practice MCQs on Bloom's Taxonomy — the six levels of the cognitive domain, their action verbs, the 2001 revised taxonomy and how teachers use it in lesson plans and test papers. They are practice material written by MR Academy for PST, JEST, SST, EST and teaching-licence test preparation, not official past-paper questions. 1. Bloom's taxonomy is a classification of:

Answer: A. Benjamin Bloom's 1956 taxonomy classifies educational objectives related to thinking (the cognitive domain), from simple recall to complex judgement.

2. How many levels are there in Bloom's cognitive taxonomy?

Answer: B. Both the original 1956 taxonomy and the revised 2001 taxonomy have six levels.

3. In the ORIGINAL Bloom's taxonomy, the lowest level is called:

Answer: C. Bloom's 1956 first level is Knowledge — the recall of facts, terms and basic concepts.

4. In the REVISED taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001), the lowest level is called:

Answer: D. The revised taxonomy renamed the levels as verbs; Knowledge became Remembering.

5. The top level of the REVISED taxonomy is:

Answer: A. In the 2001 revision, Creating — the old Synthesis, renamed and moved up — is the highest level: producing something new.

6. In the ORIGINAL taxonomy, the top (highest) level is:

Answer: C. Bloom's original order ends with Evaluation — making judgements about ideas based on criteria.

7. Which verb belongs to the Remembering level?

Answer: B. "List" asks only for recall of stored information — Remembering. Justify belongs to Evaluating, design to Creating, and compare to Understanding.

8. A student explains the water cycle correctly in his own words. This shows:

Answer: D. Explaining or restating something in one's own words is the sign of Understanding (the old Comprehension level).

9. Using a maths formula to solve a new problem is an example of:

Answer: C. Applying means using learned knowledge or a procedure in a new, concrete situation.

10. Breaking a story into its parts — plot, characters and setting — to study how they relate is:

Answer: A. Analysing means breaking material into its parts and seeing how the parts relate to each other and to the whole.

11. Judging which of two essays is better, using a marking rubric, is:

Answer: D. Evaluating means making judgements based on criteria and standards — here, the marking rubric.

12. Writing an original poem is an example of the level called:

Answer: B. Producing an original work by putting elements together in a new way is Creating, the top of the revised taxonomy.

13. Which action verb best fits the Applying level?

Answer: A. "Solve" (a new problem) puts knowledge into action — Applying. Name is Remembering, criticise is Evaluating, invent is Creating.

14. In the revised taxonomy, "differentiating" (separating relevant from irrelevant points) is a process of:

Answer: C. Anderson and Krathwohl list differentiating, organising and attributing as the core processes of Analysing.

15. Bloom's "Taxonomy of Educational Objectives" was first published in the year:

Answer: D. Handbook I: Cognitive Domain, edited by Benjamin S. Bloom, appeared in 1956.

16. The revised taxonomy of 2001 was prepared by:

Answer: B. Lorin W. Anderson (a former student of Bloom) and David R. Krathwohl revised the taxonomy in 2001.

17. In the revised taxonomy, the level names are written as:

Answer: C. Remembering, Understanding, Applying and so on are verbs, because an objective describes what the learner does.

18. Which is the correct order (lowest to highest) in the REVISED taxonomy?

Answer: A. The revised ladder rises from simple recall to original creation in exactly this order.

19. "Summarise the chapter in five lines" mainly tests the level of:

Answer: B. Summarising requires grasping the meaning and stating it briefly — a classic Understanding process in the revised taxonomy.

20. A learner checks his own solved paper against the answer key and corrects his mistakes. The main process used is:

Answer: D. The revised taxonomy places "checking" and "critiquing" under Evaluating — judging work against a standard.

21. Which question is at the Creating level?

Answer: C. Designing an original poster means producing something new — Creating. The last option is Evaluating, and the first two are Remembering and Understanding.

22. In the revised taxonomy, "knowledge" was not dropped; it became:

Answer: A. Anderson and Krathwohl made knowledge a separate dimension with four types, crossed with the six cognitive processes in a two-way table.

23. Metacognitive knowledge means:

Answer: D. Metacognitive knowledge is awareness of how you yourself learn — for example, knowing which study strategy works best for you.

24. Knowing the steps of long division is which type of knowledge?

Answer: B. Knowing how to carry out a method or procedure, step by step, is procedural knowledge.

25. The taxonomy of the affective domain (feelings, values and attitudes) is mainly associated with:

Answer: A. Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia published the affective-domain handbook in 1964; its levels run from receiving to characterising.

26. The psychomotor domain of objectives deals with:

Answer: D. Psychomotor objectives involve bodily movement and coordination — such as writing, typing or handling laboratory apparatus (Simpson's taxonomy).

27. A teacher writes the objective: "Students will be able to classify animals into mammals and birds." In the revised taxonomy, "classify" belongs to:

Answer: B. Anderson and Krathwohl list classifying among the processes of Understanding, together with exemplifying, summarising, inferring and comparing.

28. Teachers use Bloom's taxonomy while setting a question paper mainly to:

Answer: C. With the taxonomy and a table of specifications, a paper can test recall as well as higher thinking in planned proportions.

29. Which of these verbs belongs to the Evaluating level?

Answer: A. To justify is to defend a judgement with reasons — Evaluating. Recognise is Remembering, illustrate is Understanding, and assemble (producing) points to Creating.

30. The old level "Comprehension" is called, in the revised taxonomy:

Answer: B. In the 2001 revision, Comprehension was renamed Understanding.

31. A student can recite the definition of "verb" but cannot point out a verb in a sentence. He is weak at the level of:

Answer: D. He can recall the definition (Remembering) but cannot use that knowledge in a real case — Applying needs more practice.

32. Identifying the author's purpose and bias in a newspaper editorial is an example of:

Answer: C. "Attributing" — finding the purpose, point of view or bias behind material — is one of the processes of Analysing in the revised taxonomy.

33. Higher-order thinking skills (HOTS) in the revised taxonomy are generally:

Answer: B. The top three levels demand reasoning, judgement and production, so they are grouped as higher-order thinking; the first two levels are lower-order (LOTS).

34. Which set contains ONLY Remembering-level verbs?

Answer: A. Define, list and recall all ask for retrieval of stored facts — pure Remembering. Every other set mixes in higher levels.

35. In lesson planning, writing objectives with Bloom's action verbs helps the teacher to:

Answer: C. A clear verb — "explain" rather than "list", for example — fixes the intended level, so activities and questions can be aligned with it.

36. Bloom's taxonomy is called "hierarchical" because:

Answer: D. A learner generally must remember and understand before he can apply, analyse, evaluate or create — each step rests on the ones below.

37. The test item "Name the planets of the solar system" is at which level?

Answer: B. Naming from memory is simple recall — Remembering, the first level of the taxonomy.

38. Which is an example of Evaluating in daily classroom work?

Answer: C. Choosing between options against criteria and defending the choice with reasons is evaluation.

39. In the ORIGINAL taxonomy, the third level (after Knowledge and Comprehension) is:

Answer: D. The original order is Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation — so Application is third.

40. The main change Anderson and Krathwohl made at the top of the taxonomy was:

Answer: A. Synthesis (renamed Creating) and Evaluation swapped places in 2001, so Creating became the highest level.
